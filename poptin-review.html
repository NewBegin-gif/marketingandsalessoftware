Poptin Review (2026)The Recommended Plan Has No Price

Growth & RevenueResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-25

The plan Poptin recommends does not have a price on it.

Best for: sites that want popups, forms and coupons with a genuinely usable free tier and a cheap first paid rung — $20 a month for 10,000 visitors, unlimited popups and no Poptin branding — and that are happy to run the email side elsewhere.
Who should skip it: if you want the combined email-and-popup product, because the recommended tier publishes no price at any list size; you need a second administrator or an API key, which is a $95 tier rather than a $20 one; or your traffic is measured in views rather than unique visitors, where a view-metered competitor may cost less.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Sep 2026

The page opens with a question — “What are you looking for?” — and the answer changes the whole ladder. Pick Poptins, the popup product, and every tier carries a figure. Free is $0 forever with 1,000 unique visitors a month, one domain and unlimited poptins. Basic is $20 a month billed annually ($240 a year) or $25 billed monthly, for 10,000 visitors and one domain.

Pro, marked Most Popular, is $47 annually ($564) or $59 monthly, for 50,000 visitors and four domains. Agency is $95 annually ($1,140) or $119 monthly, starting at 150,000 visitors with a dropdown that climbs through 300,000, 500,000, 750,000, one million and two million, plus unlimited domains and premium support. The stated 20% annual saving checks out at all three rungs: $60 off $300, $144 off $708 and $288 off $1,428.

Pick Emails & Poptins, the combined product, and the ladder loses its prices. Free is $0 for 250 active contacts, 500 emails and 1,000 visitors. Growth — the tier the page marks RECOMMENDED — shows the word “/month” and no amount at all, at any of the eighteen contact bands in the selector. Enterprise is quote-only in both modes. The meter is unique monthly visitors, and their FAQ defines it: anyone who loads a page carrying the snippet in the last 30 days, counted once however many sessions or pages they open — unless they clear cookies or switch device or browser, in which case they count again. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Choose Emails & Poptins, and the Growth card renders the word “/month” with nothing in front of it. That is not a slow-loading figure: the contact selector works perfectly beside it — move it and the allowance updates from 500 to 1,500 to 7,500 active contacts — while the amount stays absent at every one of the eighteen bands, and the Free card in the same row renders its $0 without trouble.

We checked three bands and inspected the card itself; there is no amount element in it. So the page invites you to size your list and then declines to tell you what sizing it costs. Switch to the popup-only product and everything is priced, which makes the omission stranger rather than more forgivable. It also means the honest advice depends on which door you came through: if you want the popups, this is a transparent, cheap tool; if you want the email marketing alongside them, the price is a conversation.

The free plan is thinner than the feature list suggests. It carries 1,000 visitors a month, which is real, but the in-app billing page marks three things with a cross rather than a tick: Unbranded, Manage accounts, and Manage users & API keys. So the free tier shows Poptin's own branding on your popups. That is normal for the category, but the pricing page lists those rows under Free without making the distinction obvious.

Branding is settled at the first paid rung, but team access is not. Basic at $20 and Pro at $47 both still carry a cross on Manage accounts and on Manage users & API keys. If you need more than one person administering this, or an API key, the tier is Agency at $95 — four and a half times the entry price for a permission rather than for volume.

And read the meter definition before you size yourself. A unique visitor is counted per browser, not per person: the same buyer on a phone and a laptop is two, and anyone who clears cookies becomes new again. Your real headroom is therefore lower than your analytics suggests.

What Poptin does

Popups, forms and coupons with a drag-and-drop editor, exit-intent and cart triggers, A/B testing, 70-plus native integrations and an email-marketing side with campaigns, automations and segments. Metered on unique monthly visitors, counted once per browser per 30 days. The popup product is fully priced: free forever for 1,000 visitors and one domain, then $20 a month billed annually for 10,000, $47 for 50,000 and four domains, and $95 for 150,000 and unlimited domains (monthly billing $25, $59, $119). The combined Emails & Poptins product publishes no price for its recommended tier at any of its eighteen contact bands. Team management and API keys arrive only on the $95 tier. Read 18 Sep 2026.

What buyers report about Poptin

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run Poptin ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Speed to a working popup without code. Reviewers describe building and publishing in minutes, with the drag-and-drop editor compared to using PowerPoint.
  • • Display rules and A/B testing at this price. Several reviewers run split tests across multiple sites and name the ability to change things on the fly as the reason they stay.
  • • Support gets credited by name, with reviewers citing individual staff for fast answers and for suggesting what else to try.
  • • The free plan carries people further than expected — one reviewer reports running on it for three years, since the start of their business.
What they complain about
  • • The stock template designs. One reviewer calls them unimpressive and simply builds their own in the editor instead.
  • • How much sits behind the paywall. Two reviewers say the free plan is limited and that the advanced features and integrations need a paid subscription, with one adding that the pricing structure will not suit every budget.
  • • A short learning curve on the design interface — described as needing time to dig in rather than as difficulty, and explicitly not requiring code.

Where the ratings disagree. SourceForge shows 5.0 overall across 13 reviews, broken down as ease 4.8, features 4.8, design 4.7 and support 5.0. Design is the lowest of the four and the template complaint above is the same point in words, so the sub-scores are doing work the headline hides. The base is small and skews positive in a specific way worth knowing: several of the 'cons' fields say the reviewer has not run into a problem yet, which is not the same as there not being one.

What the tiers buy is traffic, then permissions

On the popup product the ladder is honest and easy to read, so the sums are worth doing. Billed annually: Free $0 for 1,000 visitors a month, Basic $240 a year for 10,000, Pro $564 for 50,000, Agency $1,140 for 150,000. Per thousand visitors a month that works out at about $0.17 on Basic, $0.08 on Pro and $0.05 on Agency — so the unit price halves at each step and the ladder rewards volume properly, which is less common than it should be.

Paying monthly instead costs $25, $59 and $119, and the 20% annual saving is real at every rung: $60, $144 and $288. But volume is not the only thing the steps carry, and the second thing is easy to miss. Domains go one, one, four, unlimited — so Basic buys ten times the traffic and still covers a single site. And two permissions, Manage accounts and Manage users & API keys, are crossed out on Free, Basic and Pro alike and only appear on Agency.

A two-person marketing team that needs its own logins, or anyone who wants the API, is looking at $95 a month rather than $20, for reasons that have nothing to do with how much traffic they have. Agency also behaves differently from the tiers below it: its visitor allowance is a dropdown rather than a fixed number, running 150,000, 300,000, 500,000, 750,000, one million, two million. The page does not show what the higher steps cost, so $95 is the floor of that tier rather than its price.

On the combined Emails & Poptins product there is nothing to weigh, because Growth carries no figure. That is not a judgement about value — it is that the comparison cannot be made from anything Poptin publishes.

How to actually use Poptin

Decide which of the two products you are buying before anything else, because the pricing page is really two pages behind one toggle. Poptins on its own is fully priced and cheap. Emails & Poptins, the combined product, publishes no figure for its recommended tier, so going that way means asking for a quote rather than reading one. Start on the free plan, which is unusually usable: 1,000 unique visitors a month, one domain and unlimited popups, permanently.

What it does not include is the removal of Poptin's branding — the in-app billing page marks Unbranded with a cross on Free — so treat it as a real test rather than a permanent home if the popups are customer-facing. Size yourself on their definition of a visitor, not on your own. Their FAQ counts anyone who loads a page carrying the snippet within 30 days, once per browser: the same person on a phone and a laptop is two visitors, and clearing cookies makes them new again.

Take your analytics figure and assume the Poptin number will be higher. Check the domain count against your sites before you pick a tier. Free and Basic each cover one domain, Pro covers four, and only Agency is unlimited — so a second site can move you up a rung for reasons unrelated to traffic. And if more than one person will administer this, or you want an API key, look straight at Agency.

Manage accounts and Manage users & API keys are crossed out on Free, Basic and Pro, which makes $95 a month the real entry price for a team rather than $20.

Frequently asked questions

How does Poptin decide I have used up my visitors?

Their own FAQ, read on poptin.com/pricing on 19 September 2026, defines it: a unique monthly visitor is anyone who loads one of your pages carrying the Poptin snippet in the last 30 days. One person opening five sessions and twenty pages still counts once — but clearing cookies, switching browser or picking up a different device makes them count again. Three things follow from that. The meter is browsers over a rolling 30 days, not people. It counts page loads carrying the snippet, not popups shown, so a page where nothing ever fires still spends the allowance. And your analytics figure will understate it, because the same person on a phone and a laptop is two visitors here. Put the snippet only where you actually want a popup, and assume Poptin's count runs above your own.

On the email side, what counts as an active contact?

The same FAQ, read 19 September 2026: a contact is active if it held the status Subscribed or Unconfirmed at least once during the current billing cycle. It is counted once per cycle however often its status changes, however many emails it receives, and however many subaccounts it appears in — and, the line worth reading twice, it still counts for that cycle even if the person unsubscribes inside it. So an unconfirmed address that never opts in and a subscriber who leaves in week one both occupy a slot until the cycle rolls over. Clean the list before a cycle starts rather than during it.

Can I run Poptin for clients, and what does that take?

Yes, and the FAQ names the tier: sub-accounts — separate popups, emails and automations per client, site or project — come with the Agency or Growth plans, and only an Agency plan lets you create and manage user accounts for clients. On the popup-only ladder that matters more than traffic does. The pricing page marks Manage accounts and Manage users crossed out on Free, Basic and Pro, and ticked only on Agency, so the real entry price for working on behalf of other people is Agency at $95 a month billed annually ($1,140 a year, or $119 monthly) rather than Basic at $20 — for reasons that have nothing to do with how many visitors you have. If referring rather than reselling is what you meant, the FAQ puts the affiliate programme at a lifetime 25% monthly commission per new paying member.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
