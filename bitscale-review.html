Bitscale Review (2026)200 Free Credits, Then $349/mo

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

The free tier is real and needs no card, which makes Bitscale unusually easy to judge for yourself before paying.

Best for

sales and RevOps teams that already run enrichment across several vendors and want the waterfall, the signals and the CRM sync in one place, with enough monthly volume to put 15,000 credits to work.

Skip it if

you enrich a few hundred records a month -- the free 200 credits or a per-lookup tool costs a fraction -- or you need a flat, predictable bill, because credits are consumption and consumption moves with your prospecting.

We tested it · 2026-09-11

Daan created the free account and logged in; everything after that was read inside the workspace on 11 September 2026 -- the credit screen, the plans and billing screen and the upgrade panel -- alongside Bitscale's own credit documentation. Two limits worth stating plainly. We did not run an enrichment: the grid renders in a canvas our browser panel could not drive, so the per-record credit costs below come from Bitscale's published credit table and not from a meter we moved ourselves. And Bitscale is an affiliate partner of ours; their partner manager offered 2,000 bonus credits that same morning, which had not been added when we looked -- the account was on the ordinary free allowance of 200, which is what a new reader gets.

  • The free tier runs two meters, not one, and the account screen shows both: 200 platform credits and 2,000 search credits, each resetting monthly, with the next search reset dated on the page. Enriching a record spends platform credits; finding one through Find People or Find Companies spends a search credit, one per record. A reader budgeting on "200 credits" is reading half the meter, and it is the half that runs out first.
  • On Free, nothing carries over. The billing screen states it twice -- "Rolled-Over Credits: Not included in the plan" and "Rollover Limit: Not included in the plan" -- so an unused month is gone rather than banked. Rollover is listed among the things the first paid plan unlocks, next to credit rollover, the 100+ providers, action columns beyond 20 per grid, and the inbound integrations (RB2B, Factors, Vector).
  • What one enriched record costs is published, and the range is the whole story: one to twelve platform credits per successful email or phone lookup, depending on how many providers the waterfall is allowed to try. An AI enrichment is one to two credits a run, a BitAgent website scrape one per query, and anything arriving through the API is free. On Growth that turns 15,000 credits into anywhere between 1,250 and 15,000 enriched contacts -- 2.3 cents each at one credit, 28 cents at twelve.

The meter is credits. Free is $0 with 200 credits, no card required, capped at 20 action columns per grid and 50,000 rows. Growth is $349 a month for 15,000 credits, 30 action columns and 100,000 rows, with a two-way HubSpot integration and inbound integrations like RB2B, Factors and Vector. Enterprise is quote-only but publishes a floor most vendors hide: annual billing at $15,000 and above, adding SOC 2 and SSO, priority support, role-based team access and Snowflake pipelines. Annual billing takes 10% off. Two lines in the plan table change the arithmetic and are easy to miss. Credits roll over up to three times the plan allowance on Growth and Booster, and not at all on Free, so a quiet month is banked rather than lost.

Full pricing notes (4 more paragraphs)

And a credit is spent only when a data provider returns a valid result -- the pricing FAQ says failed lookups, formula columns and CRM syncs are always free -- so a waterfall that finds nothing costs nothing. The page also contradicts itself twice: the comparison table gives all four tiers 100+ data sources while the Free card says 10+ B2B data sources, and the Growth and Booster cards both promise Unlimited Actions while the same page's table caps action columns per grid at 30 and 50.

The step from Free to Growth is still the one to plan for: 200 credits to 15,000 with nothing in between, so the bill goes from nothing to $4,188 a year in one move. Monthly company and people search is metered separately at 2,000 on Free, 15,000 on Growth and 50,000 on Booster. Two things the pricing page does not tell you, both read on 11 September 2026.

First, what a credit actually buys: Bitscale's own credit documentation prices a found email or phone number at one to twelve platform credits per successful result, depending on how many providers the waterfall is allowed to try, with an AI enrichment at one to two credits a run and a BitAgent website scrape at one. Calls that come in through the API cost nothing.

Second, the free tier runs two meters rather than one, and the account screen shows both: 200 platform credits for enrichment and a separate 2,000 search credits for Find People and Find Companies, each resetting monthly. The upgrade panel inside the product also carries a Growth benefit the plan cards leave out -- three hours a quarter with a GTM engineer -- and states plainly what Free locks: credit rollover, more than 20 action columns per grid, the 100+ providers and the inbound integrations.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

What to check during that trial is not the feature list but your own burn rate, and Bitscale's credit table tells you the range in advance: one to twelve credits for every email or phone the waterfall finds, depending on how many providers you let it try. That is the whole budget question. The same 15,000 credits on Growth are 15,000 enriched contacts at one credit and 1,250 at twelve, so the plan you buy matters less than the depth you configure.

Run fifty real records on the free 200 credits, read the meter before and after, and multiply by your monthly volume -- that number, not the plan price, is your bill. One free-tier number is worth holding against the other. The plan table gives Free a 50,000-row grid, while Bitscale's own onboarding mail says the free plan scores up to 200 rows at a time -- and both are true, because a Score column, which grades every row against an ideal-customer profile you describe in plain English, is an AI enrichment at one to two credits a run.

The grid is not the ceiling, the 200 credits are. Finding the names draws on the separate pool of 2,000 search credits, so building the list is the cheap half; asking the product to judge it is the half that runs out.

What buyers report about Bitscale

Hands-on: tested 2026-09-11Reported by buyers: G2 listing g2, read September 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We tested Bitscale ourselves on 2026-09-11, but one test is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • What reviewers single out is not the enrichment but the waterfall's transparency. A small-business marketer writing in September 2026 says the model shows him exactly which provider returned an email address and which did not, and values being able to supply his own API keys so he can add any source he already pays for.
  • • Agency users describe it as the layer before the sender rather than a sender. A founder reviewing in June 2026 lists the jobs he actually runs through it — finding LinkedIn profiles from email addresses, separating personal from professional addresses, reading job titles, recent hires, funding signals and open postings — and says the value is turning messy lists into segments a campaign can use.
  • • Onboarding is praised as real work rather than a demo: the same founder says his first call was not a product walkthrough but a session on his own use case, with a named person from the team working through how to solve it.
  • • Reviewers name the outbound tools they connect it to — Instantly, SmartLead, Apollo — and describe the spreadsheet-style grid as something they set up without help.
What they complain about
  • • The one operational complaint that repeats is the meter, and it is the same thing our own pricing note flags: a reviewer says the credit system can feel limiting for high-volume users, and that occasional enrichment misses still need checking by hand.
  • • Speed at volume is the second. One reviewer reports that the API and the enrichments seem a little slow at times, and G2's own tag list carries a single performance-issues flag against seven positive tags.
  • • Setup is front-loaded on field mapping. A reviewer describes having to decide which CRM fields to enrich and keep in sync before day-to-day use becomes smooth, and expects new users to need time to structure their enrichment rules and CRM sync so nothing drifts.
  • • The rest of the criticism is wishes rather than faults: a higher monthly limit on the Find People search, native email validation inside the product, more native integrations, and automatic weekly monitoring of a saved company list for buying signals.

Where the ratings disagree. One platform carries the whole record, and it is lopsided: 35 of 40 reviews are five stars and none sits below three, so the listing shows a buyer almost nothing about how Bitscale fails. Two things shape that. The reviewers are overwhelmingly small-business and agency users rather than mid-market teams, and several say plainly that they are early — still early to use it, a few months now — so the set measures first impressions rather than a year of billing. The one criticism that does recur, credit burn at volume, is also the one you can measure yourself on the free tier before paying anything.

When the paid plan is worth it

The plan price is not the meter -- credits are, and the tiers buy them in bulk. Growth is $349 for 15,000 credits, Booster $799 for 50,000. Per credit that is roughly 2.3 cents against 1.6 cents, so Booster is about 30% cheaper per unit and the upgrade pays for itself the moment you would otherwise buy credits twice. Two things soften that meter, and both arrive with the first paid plan: credits roll over up to three times your plan allowance on Growth and Booster but not on Free, and a credit is spent only when a provider returns a valid result, so failed lookups, formula columns and CRM syncs cost nothing.

Paying does not only buy volume, it stops you paying for misses and for quiet months. What upgrading does not fix is the shape of the ladder. There is nothing between 200 credits and 15,000, so a team that outgrows the free tier has one move and it costs $4,188 a year, and no tier makes the enrichment itself cheaper. One feature step decides this before any of that arithmetic does: Salesforce integration sits on Booster, not on Growth.

Put the credit table against the plan and the range is wider than any tier difference. At one to twelve credits per found email or phone, Growth's 15,000 credits are somewhere between 1,250 and 15,000 enriched contacts -- two and a third cents each at best, twenty-eight cents at worst, on the same $349. What decides where you land is how many providers you let the waterfall try, which is the feature the product is sold on.

Nothing in the plan you buy moves that number; only how you configure the waterfall does.

The first findings are at the top of this page. The rest:

How to actually use Bitscale

Start on the free tier and do not build anything yet. Take fifty real records from your CRM, run one waterfall over them, and count the credits it consumed -- that number, multiplied by your monthly volume, is your actual bill and it is the only figure that matters. Count it honestly, because the meter is narrower than it looks: credits are charged only when a provider returns a valid result, so a waterfall that tries three sources and finds nothing costs you nothing, and formula columns and CRM syncs are free too.

Then check whether your CRM is on the tier you were planning to buy, since Salesforce sync arrives at Booster and not at Growth. If Attio is your system of record, the plan table answers nothing at all: Bitscale documents a native two-way Attio integration -- connected over OAuth with no API key, pulling people, companies and deals into a grid, traversing the relationships between them and pushing updates back -- but the published pricing page names only the HubSpot integration on Growth and Salesforce on Booster, and files custom CRM integrations under Enterprise.

So ask which tier carries Attio before you buy a plan for it (docs.bitscale.ai/ingredients/integrations-attio, read 15 September 2026). Only after both of those would we connect anything to production data. If you do go paid, note that unused credits roll over up to three times your plan allowance on Growth and Booster, so a slow month is banked rather than burned -- which makes the 10% annual discount easier to judge than it first looks.

Expect that count to land somewhere between one and twelve credits per found contact: that is Bitscale's own published range, and where you fall inside it depends entirely on how many providers the waterfall is allowed to try. Read the second meter too. Finding the leads draws on a separate pool of search credits -- 2,000 a month on Free, one per record from Find People or Find Companies -- so building a list and enriching it spend different budgets, and only the enrichment pool is the one that runs out.

One more enrichment is worth knowing about before you budget, because it answers a different question than the waterfall does. Bitscale documents a BuiltWith-powered tech stack lookup that reads the frameworks, platforms and tools a company actually runs -- React or Django, AWS or Shopify, HubSpot or Marketo -- so you can target on how a company operates rather than on the industry label it gives itself (docs.bitscale.ai/ingredients/tools-buildwith, read 18 September 2026).

It is an ordinary enrichment column, so it draws on the same credit pool as everything else: add it to the fifty-record count above rather than treating it as free.
