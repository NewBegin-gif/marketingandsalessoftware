The app was installed on our Shopify development store, which we set up, and our AI assistant ran the test in our browser. On 17 November 2026 the session tested UpsellPlus on a Shopify development store: the install without a payment or approval screen, one Smart cross-sell play for the cart drawer with 10% off that gave a preview about 4 minutes and about 6 clicks after the install click (The Inventory Not Tracked Snowboard $949.95 to $854.95, Gift Card $10.00 to $9.00), the Billing page with nine revenue steps from Basic $49 to Unlimited $999 a month, and the uninstall, which asks for a reason. What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The $49 buys offers and features, not headroom: headroom starts at $119. And crossing a ceiling is not something you decide: their FAQ says you are automatically upgraded to the next tier so the offers keep running, so a good week moves the invoice on its own. Read the feature list closely too, because the tier named Shopify Plus is not the same thing as the requirement: their FAQ says Plus is needed only for checkout customization and checkout upsells, so on a standard Shopify store the checkout half of a checkout-upsell app is not what you are buying.

The last thing to know is at the top, and it is not on their pricing page. The plan card says Scale stops at $5,000 a month while the comparison table on that same page says Unlimited; we asked, and UpsellPlus support confirmed on 21 September 2026 that Scale is a revenue ladder - $199 up to $5,000 of upsell revenue a month, $299 up to $10,000, $399 up to $15,000, $499 up to $20,000, $699 up to $30,000, $799 up to $40,000, and an Unlimited plan at $999 with no ceiling - and that passing $5,000 moves a store to the $299 tier on its own.

That is better news than the Enterprise tier above it suggests, but it means the number you are quoted at signup is the floor of a ladder whose higher rungs are not published anywhere.

What they praise
  • • The checkout-level offer is what merchants say they came for and could not get elsewhere: a US food brand wanted a gift auto-added to the cart only at checkout and reports failing to find another app that allowed it.
  • • The team joins calls to build the thing rather than pointing at documentation: the same merchant says UpsellPlus hopped on a call to customise the feature, and an apparel brand describes brand feedback on the cart being executed quickly.
  • • One support name recurs across independent reviews: Santiago, described as fast, professional and solving a problem in minutes.
  • • A Mexican merchant at six months makes the point that matters for post-purchase offers specifically: that they integrate with the buying experience without being invasive, which is the usual failure mode of the category.
What they complain about
  • • There are none on this platform, and that is the finding rather than an endorsement: 143 reviews and not one below five stars. The listing therefore gives a buyer no view at all of how the app fails, which is the thing you would most want from three figures' worth of reviews.
  • • The review set also skews to the beginning of the relationship. Alongside merchants at eight months, nine months and about a year sit reviews written after two hours, two days, four days and six days: enthusiasm about onboarding, before a post-purchase funnel has had time to show what it does to refunds, support load or repeat purchase.
  • • Nobody in the set quantifies the lift. Reviewers say the app increased average order value and call it an easy win, but the numbers behind that are not in the reviews, so the platform supports the direction and not the size of the effect.

Where the ratings disagree. A perfect 5.0 over 143 reviews is not evidence that nothing goes wrong; it is evidence that nobody dissatisfied has posted here. Set it against the neighbours in this same category and the contrast is instructive rather than damning: Easy Bundles carries 13 one-star reviews in 1,272, Evey Events 24 in 321. Those minorities are what let a buyer see the failure modes before hitting them. With UpsellPlus you get the upside in detail and have to find the downside yourself, so weight the free trial and your own first month more heavily than the score.

What the money actually buys

The ladder is priced by how much revenue the app is allowed to generate for you: $500 a month on Free and on Launch at $49, $2,000 on the $119 tier, $5,000 on Scale at $199. Read that column before the feature lists, because the ceilings differ far more than the features do. What $49 adds over free is offers and tooling: cart, pop-up and drawer upsells, product page and post-purchase offers, multivariate A/B testing, progress bars and free gifts, with a 14-day trial.

What $119 adds is the checkout: checkout customization, checkout upsells, thank-you page customization and AI recommendations, plus four times the revenue ceiling. Scale at $199 adds live support and a $5,000 ceiling. Annual billing takes 15% off Launch ($500 a year) and 20% off the two upper tiers ($1,149 and $1,899). What happens when you outgrow $5,000 a month is not on the listing or the pricing page, so we asked.

UpsellPlus support answered on 21 September 2026 that Scale keeps climbing in published steps - $199 up to $5,000 of upsell revenue a month, $299 up to $10,000, $399 up to $15,000, $499 up to $20,000, $699 up to $30,000, $799 up to $40,000, and an Unlimited plan at $999 with no ceiling - and that crossing $5,000 moves you to $299 automatically. Budget against that ladder rather than against the $199 on the card, because the app decides when you step up, not you.

    What happens when I pass the revenue ceiling on my plan?

    You are moved up a tier and the bill follows, without you doing anything. Their FAQ states it plainly: hit the upsell revenue limit and you automatically upgrade to the next tier so that your upsells are not interrupted. Read alongside the ladder, that is the whole pricing model in one sentence: $500 a month of upsell revenue on Free and on the $49 Launch plan, $2,000 on the $119 tier, $5,000 on Scale at $199. Crossing $500 in a good week therefore moves you from free to $49 or from $49 to $119 on its own. It is a defensible design, since the alternative is switching your offers off mid-month, but it means the invoice is driven by your own success and not by a decision you make.

    Is Scale capped at $5,000 a month or is it unlimited?

    Their own pricing page says both, and we could not resolve it on 19 September 2026. The Scale plan card says up to $5,000 a month in upsell revenue, and the App Store listing agrees. The comparison table further down that same page puts Scale in the 'Unlimited' column for the upsell revenue limit. One of the two is wrong, and the difference decides what happens to a store doing $8,000 of upsell revenue a month: either it sits on Scale at $199 or it lands in Enterprise pricing. Given that the tier below auto-upgrades when its ceiling is crossed, ask which it is in writing before you build around the $199 number. The app's own table, read on 17 November 2026, shows the steps above $199 as Scale 1 to Scale 5 and Unlimited at $999.

    Does the App Store listing show the whole price ladder?

    No: it stops one tier short. The listing shows Free, Launch at $49 a month or $500 a year, the tier named Shopify Plus at $119 or $1,149, and Scale at $199 or $1,899. The vendor's own pricing page carries a fifth option the listing never mentions: Enterprise, with no published price, sold through a demo and described as a custom plan with an audit, a dedicated customer success manager and monthly performance reviews. The same page also prices a Managed Service at an extra $499 a month on top of Scale. If you are sizing this against a competitor on listing prices alone, you are comparing against a ladder that is missing its top and its add-on.

    The $119 tier is called Shopify Plus. Do I need to be on Shopify Plus to use this?

    Not for most of it, and their FAQ is unusually direct about why. It says Shopify Plus is not required except when you want to customize your checkout with recommendations and customizations, that this is a Shopify restriction they cannot get around, and that offers on the cart page, product page, cart drawer, thank-you page and post-purchase page all work without it. So a standard Shopify store can buy the $119 tier for its higher revenue ceiling and its AI recommendations, and simply not get the checkout half. The Scale tier at $199 does state Shopify Plus is required. The tier name is the confusing part, not the rule. On 17 November 2026 the listing and the app call this $119 tier Pro, not Shopify Plus; the checkout limits are unchanged.
