E-commerceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

Read the caps before you read the prices, because the caps are what you are actually buying.

Best for: Amazon and Walmart sellers past the hobby stage who compete for the Buy Box on a catalog of up to a few thousand SKUs, and want AI repricing and deep seller analytics in one place. The 15-day no-card trial makes it cheap to test against your own listings before any commitment.

There are four tiers, and the axis that sets your price is capacity, not features. All four list the same six feature groups: Amazon & Walmart, AI repricing technology, min & max calculation, analytics, connectivity and integrations. The four tiers are set out above; what the page does not tell you is what each step actually buys. Accelerator doubles your revenue ceiling and leaves the SKU count alone, and Standard does the reverse — fifteen times the catalogue with the ceiling removed — while the seller ID and user counts never move at all.

So the middle tier is for turnover and the top tier is for range, and a shop that needs neither is paying for the other one. The revenue ceilings themselves are printed without any period attached; Seller Snap told us in writing on 24 Jul 2026 that both are calculated per month, so read them as $15,000 and $30,000 a month. Annual billing saves a flat $900 a year on both Accelerator and Standard — the same money, but 30 percent off the smaller plan against 15 percent off the larger one.

Every tier carries a 15-day trial with no credit card, and in the monthly view each price is labelled 'Starting at'. What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Two of them deserve scrutiny. First, the revenue limits — $15,000 on Starter, $30,000 on Accelerator — are printed with no period attached on the pricing page. We asked, and Seller Snap confirmed to us in writing on 24 Jul 2026 that both caps are calculated per month. That is the reading that costs you money. $15,000 a month is roughly $500 a day, so a seller who already clears that sits above Starter before signing up — and $30,000 a month puts the ceiling of Accelerator within reach of a shop doing $1,000 a day.

Measure your own monthly revenue against the tier first, because the page will not do it for you. Second, the SKU limit sits at 1,000 on both of the lower tiers, so catalog growth alone — not revenue, not users — can push you up a step. And the steps are steep: leaving Accelerator for Standard means $425/month annually or $500 monthly, roughly 2.4 times what you were paying, for capacity rather than for any feature you did not already have.

The cheapest door is also not quite $100: Starter is annual-billing only, so the lowest month-to-month entry into the product is $250/month. Finally, 'Starting at' appears above every monthly price on the vendor's own page — treat all of these as floors and confirm your own quote. Worth checking first, though: Amazon already includes a rule-based repricer. Automate Pricing comes with the Professional selling plan and supports rules such as Competitive Featured Offer and Competitive Lowest Price with a seller-set price floor, so if your problem is simply matching the competitive price without going below cost, test that before paying for anything.

And note what sets your tier: their FAQ says pricing follows the total number of SKUs in your Seller Central account, not the active ones, so a dormant catalogue costs you money.

What Seller Snap does

AI repricer and seller-analytics platform for Amazon and Walmart — what the vendor calls game-theory repricing, aimed at Buy Box share rather than racing to the bottom, plus min/max profit calculation, 80+ analytics data points and imports from tools like InventoryLab and SkuVault. Starter is $100/month billed annually (annual only); month-to-month starts at $250. 15-day free trial, no credit card. A July 2026 interface release adds live Buy Box insight with SKU-level filters, multi-store management from one screen including bulk cost updates, repricing from mobile, historical inventory-value tracking, saved views, customizable columns and a dark mode.

When the paid plan is worth it

Work out which cap binds you before you look at the prices, because the tiers move different levers. Starter at $100/month buys one marketplace, one user and 1,000 SKUs — and only on annual billing, so the cheapest month-to-month door into the product is Accelerator at $250. Accelerator adds two users and lifts the revenue cap from $15,000 to $30,000. Both figures are monthly, which Seller Snap confirmed to us in writing on 24 Jul 2026.

But it keeps the same 1,000-SKU ceiling, so it buys you nothing if catalogue size is what you keep hitting. For that, Standard is the tier that moves the wall, to 15,000 SKUs with no revenue cap. Above that, or with a second Seller ID, no price is published at all — that is Custom, and only sales knows the number. One thing no tier fixes: their FAQ states pricing follows the total number of SKUs in your Seller Central account rather than the active ones, so a large dormant catalogue is billed as if you were selling it.

How to actually use Seller Snap

Start on the 15-day trial — it needs no credit card — and stay on monthly billing until the tool has proven itself on your own listings, because Seller Snap states that annual plans cannot be cancelled during the 12-month period. Before you sign up, count two things in Seller Central: your total SKUs, all of them rather than the active ones, and your Seller IDs. Those two numbers decide your tier, so an estimate built from the headline price will be wrong if your catalogue is bigger than it feels.

Set your minimum prices before you let any repricer touch a listing; that floor is the safety rail the whole category depends on. Two limits the vendor states plainly: no eBay support, and no repricing of used items. Billing runs in advance and renews automatically, and cancelling takes effect at the end of the paid period with no refund for the unused part, so put the renewal date in your own calendar.
