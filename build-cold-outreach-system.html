Step by step

  1. 1

    Set up a separate sending domain

    Warm it up first — never send cold mail from your primary domain, or you put your real reputation at risk.

  2. 2

    Build a targeted list

    The right people only: role, company size, a real trigger. Quality over quantity, every single time.

  3. 3

    Write a short, human sequence

    3-4 steps, one clear ask, no walls of text. Sound like a person, not a template.

  4. 4

    Send with throttling & track

    Ramp volume slowly, watch your reply rates, and double down on what's working.

  5. 5

    Work replies in a CRM

    Move every reply into your pipeline so no warm lead ever goes cold.

What it roughly costs

The cheap parts are warm-up and a CRM; the cost that scales is your sending tool and data (by volume and seats). A starter setup typically runs in the low hundreds per month — always check each tool's live pricing, since plans change. The bigger investment is your time on targeting and copy, not the software.

FAQ

Does cold email still work in 2026?

Yes, when it's done right: a warmed-up separate domain, a tightly targeted list, and a short human sequence. Spray-and-pray from your main domain is what stopped working.

How do I stop my cold emails going to spam?

Send from a separate domain, warm it up first (a tool like InboxAlly builds inbox reputation), keep volume sane, and avoid spammy copy and too many links. Deliverability is most of the result.

What's the cheapest way to start?

Begin with one sending tool, a small verified list and a low-cost CRM. Spend on more data and volume only once a simple sequence is actually getting replies.

Do I need a CRM for cold outreach?

Once replies start coming in, yes — otherwise warm leads slip away. A visual pipeline like Pipedrive or an outbound-focused CRM like Close keeps follow-ups on track.
