CRM for a small sales team: what you are really choosing between

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

All three will hold your deals. What separates them is what they assume you will buy next.

Choosing a CRM feels like choosing a database and turns out to be choosing a direction. One of these expects to become your marketing platform too, one expects you to keep marketing somewhere else, and one expects your team to spend the day on the phone. Pick against that grain and you will spend a year working around the product.

The seat price is the least interesting number here. What matters is the step you take when the free tier runs out or a second team wants in, because that step is rarely small and it is very hard to reverse once years of history live in the system.

How we picked

  • Prices read on the vendor’s own page and dated, with annual and monthly given separately where they differ — in this category the gap is large.
  • We look at what the entry tier leaves out rather than what it includes, since that is what decides when you are forced to upgrade.
  • We say who should skip each one. A CRM you outgrow in a year is more expensive than one that cost more on day one.

The picks

1H
HubSpot
Best for: teams that want marketing, sales, service and content on one contact database and will actually use more than one hub —

The one that starts genuinely free — two users and a thousand marketing contacts with no time limit — and is designed so you keep adding hubs. That is its strength and its risk: the shared contact database is worth real money if you use more than one hub, and poor value if you only ever wanted a pipeline.

Skip it if you need one job done well and cheaply, because a dedicated tool will almost always beat the equivalent HubSpot hub on price; or your contact list is growing fast and your budget is not, since contacts are the meter that moves without you touching anything

2P
Pipedrive
Best for: sales teams that want a simple, visual pipeline they can use on day one, with a separate marketing tool already in place

The straightforward pipeline tool. It assumes your marketing lives somewhere else and does not try to win that argument, which makes it the easy answer for a team that wants deals visible on day one. If you want automation and outbound in the same product, this is not it.

Skip it if you want marketing automation in the same tool (HubSpot) or need built-in outbound prospecting

3C
Close
Best for: inside-sales teams that live on the phone and want calling, SMS and CRM in one place

Built around the phone: calling, SMS and CRM in one inbox, priced per seat with a meaningful annual discount. Worth the premium if your team actually dials; if your sales are email-led or self-serve you are paying for a telephony stack you will not touch.

Skip it if your sales are mostly email or self-serve — a cheaper CRM without built-in calling covers you

The free tier is a decision, not a trial

A free CRM tier is where your company history starts accumulating, and that history is what makes migrating painful later. Before you adopt one on the grounds that it costs nothing, look at the plan above it and ask whether you would be happy paying that in a year — because by then moving will cost more than the difference.

The related trap is contact-based pricing. Seats grow slowly and predictably; contact counts grow every time marketing does its job. If the meter is contacts, model it against your list in eighteen months, not today.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a CRM if we are three people?

Probably yes, but not an expensive one. The value at that size is having one place where the next step on each deal is written down; the automation and reporting that justify higher tiers only pay off with more people and more deals.

Can I run marketing from my CRM?

You can, and whether you should depends on whether you will use it. A combined platform is good value when two teams share the contact database and poor value when one team uses a fifth of the product.

How hard is it to switch later?

The data moves; the workflows, integrations and habits do not. Assume a switch costs weeks of attention rather than an export and an import, and let that push you to think one step ahead now.
