What buyers report about Restream

Hands-on: tested 2026-11-06Reported by buyers: GetApp, Capterra and Software Advice, read August 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We tested Restream ourselves on 2026-11-06, but one test is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Streaming to many destinations from one dashboard, with a usable free plan: that is the reason it exists and it works.
  • • Standard on annual billing is cheap for multistreaming: reviewers reach for it as the step that removes the watermark without a real commitment.
What they complain about
  • • Both the free plan and Standard cap at 720p. On a large screen that looks soft next to the 1080p everyone else is delivering.
  • • The free plan's watermark is visible to your whole audience on every platform.
  • • Facebook streaming and scheduling sit behind the paywall.
  • • The step from Professional (~$49/month) to Business (~$239-299) is steep with nothing between. One reviewer reports being refused a partial refund after platform glitches, half a year in.

The per-channel price rises as you climb

Restream's ladder is sold on destination count, and destination count gets more expensive per unit the higher you go -- the reverse of how volume pricing usually works. Free covers 2 channels with Restream branding. Standard is $190 a year, $15.83 a month, for 3 channels, or $5.28 a channel. Professional is $470 a year, $39.17 a month, for 5 channels and Full HD, or $7.83 a channel. Business is $2,390 a year, $199.17 a month, for 8 channels: $24.90 a channel, 4.7 times Standard's rate (re-verified 26 September 2026 against restream.io/pricing).

Read it as marginal cost and the shape is clearer still. Going from Standard to Professional buys two more channels for $23.34, or $11.67 each. Going from Professional to Business buys three more for $160, or $53.33 each. Nobody pays $53 a channel for channels, which means Business is bought for something else -- the website player, the 1,000 concurrent viewers and stream disconnect protection are what its card actually adds.

Ask before you assume the jump is about destinations. Then the line that catches teams out, and it is worse than a price. Standard cannot add a seat at all: the comparison table prices extra seats at $25 a month each on Professional and Business, and leaves the Free and Standard cells empty. Free and Standard include one seat; Professional and Business include two. So your second person does not cost you $25 on Standard -- they cost you the move to Professional, $39.17 a month against $15.83, and the third person is the first one you pay $25 for.

If more than one person touches the stream, headcount picks your plan before channel count does. Annual billing saves 16.7% on Standard and Business and 20.1% on Professional, so the 'Save 20%' badge is a rounding of the best case. Price this at your real destination count and your real headcount, in that order, and the sticker will rarely be the answer.

    How to actually use Restream

    Count destinations and people separately, because both are metered and the seat is the one that catches teams out. Standard includes one seat and the comparison table offers no way to add another -- extra seats are priced at $25 a month on Professional and Business, and the Free and Standard cells are empty. So the price of your second person is not $25, it is the jump to Professional; settle that before you pick a tier on channel count.

    Then test the thing that actually breaks, which is your upload. Multistreaming takes one feed and pushes it to several platforms, so your bandwidth and encoder settings decide the quality on all of them at once. Run a full-length test stream to your real destinations, at your real bitrate, from the venue you will broadcast from -- not a two-minute check from your desk. Use the free plan for that rehearsal, but know its limits: two channels and Restream branding on the output.

    Fine for a test, wrong for a launch. And check where Full HD begins, because in Restream Studio it arrives at Professional. If you broadcast from Studio and your audience expects 1080p, Standard is a different output quality and no add-on fixes it; the pricing FAQ adds that from your own encoder software there is no such restriction, though some platforms may not accept Full HD.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can you export your data from Restream?

    Exit check 2026-11-06; route excerpt: Download offered in item menu; came along (excerpts): not tested; lost (excerpts): not tested; re-import: not tested.

    Can I stream in 1080p on the Standard plan?

    Not from Restream Studio. The comparison table (read 26 September 2026) gives Free and Standard 720p HD and Professional, Business and Enterprise 1080p Full HD, and the FAQ says 1080p through Studio needs Professional. The same FAQ adds that if you stream from your own encoder software there is no such restriction, though some platforms may not support Full HD. So if you broadcast from OBS or similar, Standard at $19 a month does not cap your resolution; if you broadcast from Studio, it does.

    Does Restream record my streams on the free plan?

    No. In the comparison table (read 26 September 2026) the cloud-recordings row is ticked from Standard upward and the Free cell is empty. Standard records up to 6 hours per stream and keeps recordings for 15 days; Professional records 10 hours per stream for 15 days; Business 10 hours for 30 days. Download anything you want to keep before that window closes, because storage length is the one recording limit that does not grow until Business.

    Can I stream to a platform that isn't on Restream's list?

    Yes, from Standard up. The comparison table (read 26 September 2026) ticks Custom RTMP channels on Standard, Professional, Business and Enterprise and leaves Free empty, and the FAQ describes it as streaming to any platform that provides an RTMP URL and a stream key, naming Wowza and GoTo Webinar as examples. Each custom destination still counts against your channel limit: 3 on Standard, 5 on Professional, 8 on Business.

    Can guests add their own channels to my Restream event?

    From Standard up, per the pricing page. The Standard card (re-read 2 November 2026) lists Guest channels, 'letting your guests securely add their own channels to your event', on top of the Free features; Free does not have it. That is more than guests joining your studio: their own audiences receive the stream too. The card does not say whether guest channels count against Standard's 3 simultaneous channels; not confirmed.
