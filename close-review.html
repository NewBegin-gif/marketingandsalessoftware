Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 12 Sep 2026

Per-seat: Solo $19, Essentials $49, Growth $109 and Scale $149 per user/mo, or $9, $35, $99 and $139 billed annually — the same twelve amounts we have now read on 23 July, 3, 11 and 14 August and 12 September 2026. No free plan; the trial runs 14 days and close.com states no credit card is required, with $5 of calling and enrichment credits included, and the page carries a 30-day money-back guarantee.

AI credits are pooled per tier (500 a month on Solo, then 1,000, 1,500 and 2,000 per user up to 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000). Chloe, the AI sales agent Close now leads the page with, is free to access on every plan but spends those AI credits plus telephony as she works, so she is metered rather than included. Call minutes, SMS and dialer credits are billed separately and can meaningfully raise the real cost: outbound calls run about $0.02 a minute and phone lines start at about $1 a month.

The Call Assistant add-on is $50 a month per organisation plus $0.02 a minute, and additional organisations are $50 a month each, with Growth and Scale including one free secondary org. Premium Phone Numbers are listed as a feature with no price attached. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Correction after re-checking: (first corrected 2026-07-23) Third-party pricing articles still name Close's tiers Base, Startup, Professional and Enterprise. Those names are gone: the plans above are what Close sells, and we have now read them unchanged on 23 July, 3 August, 11 August, 14 August and 12 September 2026. One figure of our own has gone with them. We previously put Premium Phone Numbers at $19 a month per line; on 12 September 2026 the pricing page lists Premium Phone Numbers as a capability — lead-based call routing, IVR, round-robin — with no amount anywhere against it, so we no longer quote a price for it. Rented lines still start at about $1 per line per month. (re-verified 2026-09-12)

Close’s pricing page, as we read it

What Close does

Sales CRM unifying calling, email and SMS in one inbox, for teams that live on the phone. Seats run about $9 (Solo) to $139 (Scale) per user/month billed annually, and call, SMS and Power Dialer credits bill separately on top — a $99 plan can land nearer $160 once a rep dials all day.

What buyers report about Close

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026 (App Store reviews through 8 August 2026, SourceForge through September 2023). We have not run Close ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Ease of use is mentioned by 819 reviewers, close to half of everyone who wrote one. Helpful support at 482 mentions.
  • • The built-in dialer is the reason most calling teams pick it — around 4.7 on G2 across 2,010 reviews, 85% of them five stars.
  • • On SourceForge, calling, SMS and email being in the one tool without plug-ins is named repeatedly as the reason not to switch away, and support is described as answering within one to two hours and usually resolving the same working day.
  • • App Store reviewers who sell B2B SaaS say it fits them better than HubSpot or Salesforce, and one credits checking deal status on the road with closing a deal.
What they complain about
  • • Reporting is the consistent complaint. Pipeline reports are not fully customisable, and deals that skip or move back a stage do not report correctly.
  • • Custom fields yes, custom objects no — the leads/contacts/opportunities model is fixed.
  • • No native Apollo connector despite being the most common pairing; it goes through Zapier.
  • • Budget $150–250 per rep per month once the dialer tier, Call Assistant and calling minutes are in. The seat price is not the bill.
  • • The mobile app is the consistent complaint and it has not gone away: App Store reviews from May 2023 through 8 August 2026 call it glitchy, slow and crash-prone, with no real iPad layout (it runs as a blown-up iPhone view). Specific reports include audio dropping out on roughly one call in ten, a 30-minute delay before missed calls and messages sync, and texting from the app often failing to load so reps go back to the laptop.
  • • SourceForge adds occasional call lag and audio quality problems, some missing niche integrations, a learning curve from the sheer number of features, and time zones that are awkwardly handled when dialling.

Where the ratings disagree. The sharpest split is not between platforms but between devices. The same App Store reviewers who call the mobile app unreliable praise the desktop version in the same breath, and that pattern holds across three years to this month. G2 and SourceForge are written by people sitting at a desk, so the mobile problem barely registers there. If your reps work from a phone, weigh the App Store reviews heavier than the aggregate score.

When the paid plan is worth it

Close has no free plan, just a 14-day trial that does not ask for a credit card. Solo, the cheapest tier at $19 per user monthly or $9 on annual billing, caps you at one user and 10,000 leads. Essentials ($49 monthly, $35 annual) lifts both caps but still has no workflows. That is the catch: the automation most teams buy Close for, meaning workflow sequences, the power dialer and bulk email, starts at Growth ($109 monthly, $99 annual).

On the official feature table, Growth is the real starting point if you run outbound at any volume; Essentials covers pipeline tracking with straightforward calling. Scale ($149 monthly, $139 annual) adds the predictive dialer, call coaching, custom roles with unique permissions, and unlimited call-recording retention, where Solo and Essentials keep recordings 30 days and Growth 90. Role-based access itself is on every plan, so do not upgrade for that alone.

No tier removes the metered costs: outbound calls run around $0.02 per minute and phone numbers start at about $1 per month, on top of every subscription.

How to actually use Close

Close's own onboarding checklist puts process before data. Step 0 states that before you connect accounts or import data, it is critical to set the foundation: your sales process, meaning pipeline structure, lead statuses and opportunity stages. The docs frame this as the way to avoid rework later. Customizing lead records and custom fields is step 2, ahead of importing at step 3, so configure your fields first. Connecting email and calendar is step 1 and the docs put it at two minutes.

The friction sits in communications setup.

  • At step 5 the docs note you may be required by law to provide information before registering phone numbers.
  • Step 6 requires A2P 10DLC registration before you can text US numbers, which is a carrier compliance process rather than a toggle in Close.
  • Smart Views arrive at step 8, Workflows at step 9.
  • One independent walkthrough adds two self-inflicted traps: too many pipelines cause confusion when deals overlap, and a workflow that is too aggressive burns leads who would have converted with a gentler approach.

Frequently asked questions

What does Close actually cost per rep?

The seat is the smaller half of it. Read on their pricing page on 12 September 2026: Solo $19, Essentials $49, Growth $109 and Scale $149 per user per month, or $9, $35, $99 and $139 on annual billing. On top of every one of those sit metered costs the plan does not cover — outbound calls at about $0.02 a minute, phone lines from about $1 a month, the Call Assistant add-on at $50 a month per organisation plus $0.02 a minute, and $50 a month for each extra organisation beyond the one free secondary org Growth and Scale include. Chloe, the AI agent, costs nothing to switch on and then draws on your AI credits and telephony as she runs. If your team dials all day, budget the usage first and the seat second.

Has Close's price changed recently?

Not in the window we can prove. We hold monthly captures of close.com/pricing from July and August 2026 and read it again by hand on 14 August: the page was edited between the two captures — it is a different document, 20,260 characters against 20,388 — and every amount is the same in both. If you priced Close a month ago, that quote still stands.

Is there a free plan, and can I try it without a card?

There is no free plan. There is a 14-day trial and close.com states no credit card is required, with $5 of calling and enrichment credits included so you can test the dialer rather than only the pipeline. After you buy, the page carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. Read 14 August 2026.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
