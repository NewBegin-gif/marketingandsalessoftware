SEO tools: four jobs that are usually confused for one

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

These four do not compete with each other. Buying two of them makes sense; buying all four probably does not.

‘SEO tool’ covers at least four unrelated jobs: making a page technically correct, fixing a storefront’s metadata in bulk, estimating what competitors get, and describing images so they can be found at all. People buy one and are surprised it does not do the others.

Sorting them by job rather than by feature list also sorts them by who should buy. Two of these are platform-specific and irrelevant if you are on the other platform; one is a research subscription with a real price floor; one is a bulk operation you may only need once.

How we picked

  • We group by the job each tool does, not by the label the vendor uses.
  • Prices are read on the vendor’s own page and dated, with the annual and list figures separated where they differ.
  • We say plainly where the free version is enough, because in this category it often is.

Best for: WordPress site owners who want a powerful, feature-rich SEO plugin — the free version alone beats most paid rivals

The WordPress answer, and the honest note is that the free version already covers what most sites need. The paid ladder earns its money on rank tracking and content tooling rather than on unlocking basics, which means there is no urgency to upgrade until you actually want those.

Skip it if your SEO needs are basic and the free version covers them — there's no rush to PRO until you need tracking or Content AI

Best for: image-heavy sites that need alt text written back into the CMS automatically: e-commerce catalogues, publishers and long

A bulk operation, priced per image and written back into the CMS. Makes sense for a catalogue or an archive. For a few dozen images, write them yourself — you know why each picture is on the page and the model does not.

Buy the job, not the category

The fastest way to waste money here is to buy a second tool that overlaps the first by eighty percent and differs in the twenty you never use. Write down the job before you shop: fix pages, fix a storefront, research competitors, or describe images. Each has a clear answer and they are different answers.

The second discipline is to leave the free tier alone until it stops being enough. In this category the free versions are unusually capable, and upgrading early buys reporting rather than results.

Frequently asked questions

Is a paid SEO plugin worth it?

Only once you want the things the free version deliberately withholds — usually rank tracking and content assistance. The technical basics that actually move indexing are free in every serious plugin.

Are competitor traffic estimates accurate?

They are estimates, and directionally useful rather than precise. Treat them as a way to compare channels and trends, not as a number to put in a board deck without a caveat.

Does alt text help SEO?

It helps images be found and it helps people using screen readers, which is the better reason. Generated text is fine at scale; hand-written is better where the image carries meaning the model cannot see.
