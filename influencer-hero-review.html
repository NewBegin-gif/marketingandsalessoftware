Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Sep 2026

Four plans, and the page quotes all of them per month with three billing routes side by side. Billed monthly: Standard $649, Pro $1,049, Business $2,490. Billed quarterly, which is the rate our headline uses: $519, $839 and $1,992 a month, charged up front as $1,557, $2,518 and $5,976 a quarter. Billed annually: $454, $734 and $1,743 a month, charged up front as $5,452, $8,812 and $20,916 a year.

Enterprise is quote-only. Quarterly takes 20% off the monthly rate and annual 30%, and every plan carries a three-month minimum term. Standard buys 1,000 reach-outs a month, product send-outs, 20 influencer payouts, influencer analytics, consultation and training, and one seat. Pro buys 5,000 reach-outs, unlimited templates, conversion-enhanced filters, 200 payouts, a dedicated account manager and three seats. Business buys 10,000 reach-outs, UGC tracking up to 1,500, custom API integrations, unlimited payouts, unlimited follower checks and eight seats.

The influencer CRM, the outreach automation and the UGC tracking are in every plan — the ladder is gated on quota and seats, not on features. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

The honest knock

Every plan carries it, so the smallest honest entry price is not $519 but three months of it: $1,557 if you prepay the quarter, or $1,947 if you pay month by month at $649. That is the figure to weigh, because an influencer programme takes longer than four weeks to produce anything you can judge. Ask yourself whether you can fill 1,000 reach-outs a month for three months before you sign, not whether you can afford one.

The quota is the meter, and it counts new relationships rather than messages. Influencer Hero told us in writing on 18 September 2026 that a reach-out is counted once, when a creator is first contacted, and that every message after that — follow-ups, replies, the whole conversation — is unlimited and never draws the allowance down again. That is the friendlier of the two readings and it is worth knowing, because it means the meter measures how many creators you open a line to, not how diligently you chase them.

Standard's 1,000 a month against Pro's 5,000 is a fivefold jump for a 62% price increase, which is the tell that they expect volume to be the binding constraint rather than features — and the feature list confirms it: the CRM, the outreach automation and the UGC tracking are in every tier from the bottom. What the page does not publish is any price for running out. The vendor names two routes when you hit the cap — buy extra quota as a top-up, or move up a tier — and says it steers brands towards the tier, on the argument that those who top up once tend to keep topping up.

Neither the top-up nor an overage rate is priced anywhere on the pricing page, so the only number you can actually budget with is the next plan: $320 a month more from Standard to Pro on the quarterly rate, $400 on the monthly one. In a product sold on volume, an unpriced top-up is a real gap. Payouts are the second meter and the one that bites at the bottom.

Standard covers 20 influencer payouts; Pro covers 200. If your programme pays creators rather than only gifting them, 20 is a pilot, and the jump to a real programme is the jump to $839. Seats follow the same shape — one, three, eight — so a two-person team is pushed to Pro by headcount alone, regardless of volume.

Three months up front is the price, not one

Take the headline figures and turn them into what you will actually be charged, because this page quotes per month and bills by the period. Every plan has a three-month minimum. Prepay the quarter and Standard is $1,557 committed, Pro $2,518 and Business $5,976. Pay month by month instead and the same three months cost $1,947, $3,147 and $7,470, because the monthly rate is $649, $1,049 and $2,490 rather than $519, $839 and $1,992.

Paying quarterly takes 20% off, annually 30%, both charged up front for the whole period, so an annual Standard commitment is $5,452 decided once. Now the ladder itself. Standard to Pro is $320 a month more on the quarterly rate, a 62% increase, and it buys five times the reach-outs (1,000 to 5,000), ten times the payouts (20 to 200) and three seats instead of one. Measured per reach-out that is a real improvement: Standard is about 52 cents, Pro about 17 cents, Business about 20 cents.

Since the vendor counts a reach-out once per newly contacted creator rather than per message, those are the cost of opening a relationship, and follow-ups inside it are free. The cheapest one on the page is Pro's, not Business's — Business buys UGC tracking, custom API integrations, unlimited payouts and eight seats, and it costs more per creator to get them. That makes the tiering question unusually clean. If you will contact more than about 1,600 new creators a month, Pro is cheaper per reach-out than Standard is at its own cap, and you should be on it.

If you will not, the binding constraint is probably payouts or seats rather than volume, and the honest answer may be that the programme is not ready for a three-month commitment at all. What no tier buys you is a published price for running out. The vendor offers a top-up or the next plan, but neither the pricing page nor the documentation we were sent puts a figure on the top-up. Budget the tier you will grow into rather than the one you fit today.

How to actually use Influencer Hero

Decide the employment of the budget before the tier: this is a three-month commitment on every plan, so the question is whether you can run a programme for a quarter, not whether you can afford a month. Size yourself on reach-outs first, because that is the meter. Count the creators you would realistically contact for the first time in a month — the vendor counts a reach-out once, when a creator is first contacted, and leaves every follow-up and reply inside that conversation uncounted.

So the number to estimate is new names, not messages, and a small list you work hard is cheap here while a wide list you barely touch is not. Standard's 1,000 works out at about 52 cents a creator; if your number is above roughly 1,600, Pro is cheaper per creator and you should start there. Then check the two limits that catch people out at the bottom. Standard includes 20 influencer payouts a month and a single seat.

If you pay creators rather than only sending product, or if more than one person will work in the tool, those are the constraints that move you up rather than volume. Connect the affiliate side early. The platform carries creator discovery, outreach automation, a CRM, UGC tracking and affiliate management together, and the argument for paying this much rather than assembling it from cheaper parts rests on those pieces sharing one record of each creator.

Running outreach here while tracking payouts in a spreadsheet gives you the price of the suite and the value of a mailbox. One practical note on the route in: the affiliate link we publish takes you to the site. The vendor also circulates a link that opens a Calendly demo booking instead — useful if you want the walkthrough, but it is a sales call rather than a trial, and there is no self-serve sign-up published anywhere on the pricing page.
