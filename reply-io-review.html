The email track stops making sense at 5,000 contacts

Reply sells three separate tracks and the one you pick changes everything, so take them in order. Email Volume is pay-as-you-go on active contacts: $49 for 1,000, $59 for 2,000, $69 for 3,000, $89 for 5,000, $159 for 10,000, $259 for 25,000 and $459 for 50,000. Put those two side by side at 5,000 contacts and they cost the same $89 — except one of them caps you at 5,000 and the other does not cap you at all, and throws in four more channels.

Above that point the Email track is strictly the worse purchase: 10,000 contacts is $159 against $89, and 50,000 contacts is $459 against $89. That is 5.2× the money for a narrower product. The add-ons make it starker. On the Email track, LinkedIn automation is $69 a month per account and calls and SMS are $29. So the cheapest possible multichannel setup on the Email track — 1,000 contacts plus LinkedIn — is $118, already above the $89 that buys unlimited contacts with LinkedIn, SMS, calls and WhatsApp included.

Add calling and it is $147. There is no combination of Email plus add-ons that beats Multichannel, which makes the $89 plan the default and the Email track the exception. So why does the Email track exist? Because Multichannel's real limit is not contacts, it is mailboxes: ten of them on annual billing, five on monthly. Whatever daily send discipline you keep per mailbox — and cold outreach lives or dies on that discipline — ten mailboxes multiplied by it is your actual ceiling.

Work that number out for your own sending practice before you assume unlimited contacts means unlimited sending. That, and not the contact count, is where the Email track earns its place. One oddity in the volume ladder itself, for anyone staying on it: the marginal rate is a flat $0.010 per contact all the way to 5,000, then jumps to $0.014 for the 5,000-to-10,000 band, then drops to $0.0067 above that.

The single most expensive place to be on this price list is between five and ten thousand contacts — exactly the band where Multichannel is already cheaper. AI SDR is a different purchase entirely. At 3,000 contacts the Email track costs $69; the same volume through Jason is $500 to $1,000, which is seven to fifteen times more. You are not buying delivery there, you are buying the labour that writes and sequences — price it against a person's salary, not against the sending tools.

How to actually use Reply.io

Pick your track before you look at any price, because the three are different products rather than tiers. Multichannel at $89 a month billed annually gives unlimited active contacts plus LinkedIn, SMS, calls and WhatsApp. The Email Volume track is priced per active contact and is only the cheaper choice below 5,000 contacts — and only if you add nothing to it, since a single add-on takes it past $89.

Then count mailboxes rather than contacts, because that is Multichannel's real ceiling: ten on annual billing and five on monthly. Multiply by whatever daily send discipline you keep per mailbox — and cold outreach lives or dies on that discipline — and you have your actual sending capacity, which is the number the plan does not tell you. Budget the sending infrastructure separately. Domains, mailboxes and warm-up are what any cold-outreach stack needs and none of it is in the subscription, so plan on 1.5 to 2 times the sticker before the platform is genuinely working.

If AI SDR is on the table, price it against a person rather than against software. At $500 to $1,000 a month for 1,000 to 3,000 contacts, Jason costs seven to fifteen times what the same contact volume costs on the email track — because what you are buying there is the writing and the sequencing, not the sending.

Frequently asked questions

What does Reply.io cost?

It depends which of the three tracks you buy. Email Volume starts at $49 a month for 1,000 active contacts and climbs to $459 at 50,000. Multichannel is $89 a month billed annually with unlimited contacts. The Jason AI SDR track starts between $500 and $1,000 a month. Read on 17 August 2026.

Why do people say multichannel costs far more than advertised?

Because on the Email track it does: LinkedIn automation is $69 a month per account and calls and SMS $29, so bolting both onto a $49 email plan lands you near $150 before data credits. The all-inclusive Multichannel plan at $89 a month annually already contains them, which is why the cheaper-looking track is often the more expensive one. Work out which channels you actually need first.

Is the AI SDR worth ten times the price?

That is the question the price forces, and the honest answer is that it is a different purchase: $500 to $1,000 a month buys an agent that sources and researches prospects, against $89 for a tool you drive yourself. Judged as software it looks expensive; judged against the cost of an SDR it does not. Decide which of those two budgets it comes out of before comparing it with sequencing tools.

What should I weigh before buying Growth & Revenue software?

It depends on whether you have the bottleneck it solves. Small teams get the most out of this category when one clear problem is already costing real hours or revenue; buying ahead of that just adds cost and another login. Price it against the hours or lost deals it removes, not against its feature list, and start on the smallest plan that covers the job.
