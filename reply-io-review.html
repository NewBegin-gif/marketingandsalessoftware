Reply.io: what it costs and who should skip it

AI-driven sales engagement platform. Automate your cold outreach across email, LinkedIn, and calls while keeping the human touch. Now includes a mobile app (Jason AI) for iOS and Android to run outreach on the go.

What to check before you buy

The advertised $89 is the lobby, not the building: use LinkedIn and calling — the whole point of multichannel — and the real per-seat price roughly doubles. Budget 1.5-2x the sticker, plus sending infrastructure (domains, warmup) that any cold-outreach stack needs.

The platform is genuinely capable; the pricing just rewards reading this paragraph before the demo call.

The pricing, as we read it

(re-verified 2026-08-17 against Reply's own machine-readable plan breakdown) Three separate tracks, and the one you pick changes the arithmetic completely. Email Volume is pay-as-you-go by active contacts: 1,000 for $49/mo, 2,000 for $59, 3,000 for $69, 5,000 for $89, 10,000 for $159, 25,000 for $259 and 50,000 for $459, with 100,000+ quoted.

Multichannel is all-inclusive from $89/month billed annually ($99 monthly) with unlimited active contacts, adding LinkedIn, SMS, calls and WhatsApp, 10 mailboxes (5 on monthly) and 50 live data credits. AI SDR (Jason) runs $500 to $1,000 a month billed annually at Starter (1,000-3,000 contacts) and $1,500 to $3,000 at Growth (5,000-10,000), with Enterprise quoted.

Agency Core starts at $210/month and Agency AI SDR at $500 per client. Add-ons on top of the Email track: LinkedIn automation $69/month per account, calls and SMS $29/month per account, AI/live-data credits from $39/month and email validation from $20/month.

When the paid tier is worth it

Reply sells three separate tracks and the one you pick changes everything, so take them in order (re-verified 2026-08-17 against Reply's own machine-readable plan breakdown). Email Volume is pay-as-you-go on active contacts: $49 for 1,000, $59 for 2,000, $69 for 3,000, $89 for 5,000, $159 for 10,000, $259 for 25,000 and $459 for 50,000.

Multichannel is $89 a month billed annually ($99 monthly) with unlimited active contacts, adding LinkedIn, SMS, calls and WhatsApp, 10 mailboxes (5 on monthly) and 50 live data credits. Put those two side by side at 5,000 contacts and they cost the same $89 — except one of them caps you at 5,000 and the other does not cap you at all, and throws in four more channels.

Above that point the Email track is strictly the worse purchase: 10,000 contacts is $159 against $89, and 50,000 contacts is $459 against $89. That is 5.2× the money for a narrower product. The add-ons make it starker.

On the Email track, LinkedIn automation is $69 a month per account and calls and SMS are $29. So the cheapest possible multichannel setup on the Email track — 1,000 contacts plus LinkedIn — is $118, already above the $89 that buys unlimited contacts with LinkedIn, SMS, calls and WhatsApp included.

Add calling and it is $147. There is no combination of Email plus add-ons that beats Multichannel, which makes the $89 plan the default and the Email track the exception. So why does the Email track exist?

Because Multichannel's real limit is not contacts, it is mailboxes: ten of them on annual billing, five on monthly. Whatever daily send discipline you keep per mailbox — and cold outreach lives or dies on that discipline — ten mailboxes multiplied by it is your actual ceiling.

Work that number out for your own sending practice before you assume unlimited contacts means unlimited sending. That, and not the contact count, is where the Email track earns its place. One oddity in the volume ladder itself, for anyone staying on it: the marginal rate is a flat $0.010 per contact all the way to 5,000, then jumps to $0.014 for the 5,000-to-10,000 band, then drops to $0.0067 above that.

The single most expensive place to be on this price list is between five and ten thousand contacts — exactly the band where Multichannel is already cheaper. AI SDR is a different purchase entirely. Jason runs $500 to $1,000 a month billed annually at Starter (1,000-3,000 contacts) and $1,500 to $3,000 at Growth (5,000-10,000), with Enterprise quoted.

At 3,000 contacts the Email track costs $69; the same volume through Jason is $500 to $1,000, which is seven to fifteen times more. You are not buying delivery there, you are buying the labour that writes and sequences — price it against a person's salary, not against the sending tools.

Agency Core starts at $210 a month and Agency AI SDR at $500 per client.

How we would start

Pick your track before you look at any price, because the three are different products rather than tiers. Multichannel at $89 a month billed annually gives unlimited active contacts plus LinkedIn, SMS, calls and WhatsApp.

The Email Volume track is priced per active contact and is only the cheaper choice below 5,000 contacts — and only if you add nothing to it, since a single add-on takes it past $89. Then count mailboxes rather than contacts, because that is Multichannel's real ceiling: ten on annual billing and five on monthly.

Multiply by whatever daily send discipline you keep per mailbox — and cold outreach lives or dies on that discipline — and you have your actual sending capacity, which is the number the plan does not tell you. Budget the sending infrastructure separately.

Domains, mailboxes and warm-up are what any cold-outreach stack needs and none of it is in the subscription, so plan on 1.5 to 2 times the sticker before the platform is genuinely working. If AI SDR is on the table, price it against a person rather than against software.

At $500 to $1,000 a month for 1,000 to 3,000 contacts, Jason costs seven to fifteen times what the same contact volume costs on the email track — because what you are buying there is the writing and the sequencing, not the sending.

Who it is for

Best for sales teams running structured multichannel outbound (email + LinkedIn + calls) who want sequences, warmup and a B2B database in one platform.

Skip it if you only send plain email sequences (cheaper single-channel tools exist), or your list is too small to justify per-seat-plus-add-ons math.

What to weigh it against

The comparison that matters is Instantly or Apollo — Instantly is cheaper email-first, Apollo bundles a bigger database — Reply's edge is mature multichannel sequencing; compare real per-seat totals.

Questions buyers actually ask

What does Reply.io cost?

It depends which of the three tracks you buy. Email Volume starts at $49 a month for 1,000 active contacts and climbs to $459 at 50,000. Multichannel is $89 a month billed annually with unlimited contacts. The Jason AI SDR track starts between $500 and $1,000 a month. Read on 17 August 2026.

Why do people say multichannel costs far more than advertised?

Because on the Email track it does: LinkedIn automation is $69 a month per account and calls and SMS $29, so bolting both onto a $49 email plan lands you near $150 before data credits. The all-inclusive Multichannel plan at $89 a month annually already contains them, which is why the cheaper-looking track is often the more expensive one. Work out which channels you actually need first.

Is the AI SDR worth ten times the price?

That is the question the price forces, and the honest answer is that it is a different purchase: $500 to $1,000 a month buys an agent that sources and researches prospects, against $89 for a tool you drive yourself. Judged as software it looks expensive; judged against the cost of an SDR it does not. Decide which of those two budgets it comes out of before comparing it with sequencing tools.
