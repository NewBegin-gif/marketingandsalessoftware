What they complain about
  • • Billing is the single biggest theme in the negative reviews, and it has a specific shape.   • • The plan scales with how many products you link (free up to ten, then paid tiers). A June 2026 reviewer says the downgrade is behind a toggle that defaults to on, so you keep paying for products you are not selling.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read what the free tier actually is before anything else: free to install means you may link zero products to your store. It is not a usable free plan, it is an empty account. Above that the tiers are a product allowance and nothing else: the feature list is identical on all three paid plans. Beginner at $29 covers 1 to 20 linked products, Pro at $49 covers 21 to 100, Plus at $79 covers 101 or more.

Per product at each ceiling that is about $1.45, $0.49 and under $0.78, so the middle tier is the best value per line and the step to Plus only pays once you are past a hundred. Since no feature is gated, upgrading early buys you nothing at all. Where you sign up changes the ladder. Zendrop's own pricing page has no $29 tier (just Free, Pro at $49 and Plus at $79) and it is the only place the annual rates appear: $399 a year for Pro and $549 for Plus, which works out at $33.25 and $45.75 a month.

If you were going to run Pro for a year anyway, that is about $189 cheaper than twelve monthly charges. Neither figure is the bill. Product and shipping cost is charged per order on top of every plan, and the listing states external charges may be billed separately from your Shopify invoice, so the subscription is the smaller of the two lines you pay. One caution from our own test of 17 November 2026: the Beginner and Pro tiers and the free level on the listing were not offered inside the app's onboarding, which showed only Plus ($79 after a 7 day trial) and a pay as you go Flex route, so the $29 entry price may not be what a new install is shown.

    How to actually use Zendrop

    Install on the free tier first and treat it as research: you can browse the full catalogue there, but you cannot link a product, so nothing ships until you are on a paid plan. Pick the tier by counting the products you actually intend to list rather than the ones you want to try, because the ladder is priced on linked products (1-20, 21-100, 101 or more) and that count is the only thing you are choosing.

    Check both sign-up routes before you pay, because they are not the same ladder: the App Store listing has a $29 Beginner tier and no annual option, while zendrop.com sells Pro and Plus by the year at $399 and $549. Before committing, put the per-order product and shipping cost of your three best candidates next to your intended retail price: that number decides whether the store works, and no plan changes it.

    Finally, the app bills outside your Shopify invoice, so put it in whatever you use to track subscriptions instead of expecting it to appear on the Shopify bill.

    Is it cheaper to subscribe through Shopify or on Zendrop's own site?

    They are not the same ladder, which is the part worth checking before you pay. As read on 18 September and again on 5 October 2026, the App Store listing sells Beginner $29, Pro $49 and Plus $79 monthly with no annual option. zendrop.com/pricing has no $29 tier at all (Free, Pro $49 a month or $399 a year, Plus $79 a month or $549 a year) so the annual rates exist only there.     Does a higher tier get me better features or faster fulfilment?

    No, and the listing is unusually clear about it. All three paid tiers show an identical feature list (trending products, Zendrop Academy, Zendrop AI access, custom branding) and what changes is only how many products you may link: 1-20 on Beginner, 21-100 on Pro, 101 or more on Plus. Nothing about upgrading makes an order ship faster or cost less per unit. So the tier is a line-count decision, and paying for headroom you are not using buys nothing. Per product at each ceiling the rates work out at roughly $1.45, $0.49 and under $0.78, which makes Pro the best value per line and Plus worth it only once you are past a hundred products.

    How good is its track record on the App Store?

    It is the vendor's own sales channel rather than an independent panel, which is worth naming as a limit, and within that the record is strong but split.     Will Zendrop take customer returns at its own address?

    Can I still get my subscription refunded after I start fulfilling orders?
