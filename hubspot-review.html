Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 18 Aug 2026

(2026-08-18, read in a live browser on their own marketing pricing page, USD view) Free is $0 for up to 2 users and 1,000 marketing contacts, with no time limit. Starter is advertised at $7 per seat a month against a list price of $20 per seat a month, under a banner reading “Save up to 65% on Starter — new customers only, select offers available for a limited time”.

It includes 500 HubSpot Credits and 1,000 marketing contacts. Professional starts at $800 a month paid annually, or $890 a month billed monthly, and includes 3 Core Seats with additional Core Seats from $45 a month, 3,000 credits and 2,000 marketing contacts. On top of that comes a required one-time Professional Onboarding fee of $3,000 that the headline price does not include.

Enterprise starts at $3,600 a month with 5 Core Seats, extra seats from $75, 5,000 credits and 10,000 marketing contacts, and a required one-time onboarding fee of $7,000. HubSpot AEO, their answer-engine visibility tracker, is sold separately at $50 a month or $45 annually for 25 prompts across 3 engines, with a 28-day trial. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The first is the $7. It is the number in large black type on the Starter card, and the $20 beside it in small grey type is the list price. The banner above the table explains why: up to 65% off Starter, new customers only, for a limited time. So $7 is an introductory rate and $20 is what the seat costs afterwards — a 186% step for a system that by then holds your contact database, which is the single most painful kind of software to leave.

The second is the $800. Professional carries a required one-time onboarding fee of $3,000, disclosed in a footnote under the card rather than in the price. Year one on Professional is therefore $800 × 12 + $3,000 = $12,600 if you pay annually, or $890 × 12 + $3,000 = $13,680 month to month. The onboarding adds roughly a third to the first year, and no amount of shopping around removes it — it is mandatory.

Enterprise does the same thing at $7,000 on top of $43,200 a year. The third is the seat count, because it is not the meter that usually bites. Professional includes 3 Core Seats and 2,000 marketing contacts. For a marketing platform, contacts are the axis that grows on its own: your list gets bigger whether or not you hire anyone, and the contact tier moves the bill without anyone deciding to upgrade.

Price your next two years on the list you expect to have, not the team you have now. One more thing worth knowing before you start on Free. The free tier is genuinely usable — two users, 1,000 contacts, no expiry — and that is exactly how the ladder is designed to work. The step from Starter to Professional is not incremental: at list prices a three-seat team goes from $60 a month to $890 plus $3,000 once, which is roughly fifteen times the monthly cost. Whatever you build on Free, build it knowing where the next rung is.

What HubSpot does

CRM platform with marketing, sales, service and content hubs on one contact database. Free for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts; Starter is advertised at $7 per seat a month against a $20 list price, and Professional starts at $800 a month with a mandatory $3,000 onboarding fee on top.

What buyers report about HubSpot

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run HubSpot ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The consolidation is what reviewers keep: email, landing pages, workflows and analytics on one contact database, instead of stitching separate tools together.
  • • Capterra reviewers repeatedly call the interface clean and the workflow setup approachable, including people with no marketing-automation background.
  • • Automation of repetitive campaign work is credited with real time savings, and several reviewers name individual support staff as helpful.
What they complain about
  • • Capterra reviewers describe reporting and template customisation as locked down on the cheaper plans, forcing an upgrade to reach features they expected to have.
  • • Cost tracks contact volume rather than usage, so the bill rises as the list grows even when nothing else changes.
  • • On Trustpilot the complaints are commercial rather than technical: a small business billed roughly A$665 a month was auto-renewed into another twelve-month term and told early cancellation is only allowed if the company closes.
  • • One reviewer says the sales team moved them from month-to-month to an annual contract, and that product problems went unaddressed afterwards.
  • • A long-time user reports the Marketing Contact setting being removed across all their contacts when they bought a new licence, with only an AI assistant available to escalate to.
  • • Another says their account was deleted a day into a seven-day notice period and the customer data went with it.
  • • A new customer says HubSpot's own AI setup guidance started an import that burned a month's credits after 250 of 2,000 emails, and support offered no way to reset it.

Where the ratings disagree. The two platforms are answering different questions. Capterra reviewers rate the software and mostly like it; Trustpilot reviewers rate the commercial relationship — renewals, cancellation terms, billing and support escalation — and that page is dominated by one-star accounts. Trustpilot also notes HubSpot has no recent history of inviting reviews, so its sample skews towards people motivated to complain. Read together they are consistent with our own pricing section: the product is not what buyers regret, the contract is.

When the paid plan is worth it

The honest answer is that the Free tier is not a trial, it is a working product, and most small teams should stay on it far longer than HubSpot's funnel suggests. Two users and 1,000 contacts with no expiry covers a real business for a while. The question is what forces the move. Not features, usually — the contact cap. Once you pass 1,000 contacts you are buying a paid tier whether or not you wanted the tools that come with it, and at that moment the Starter promo is what you will see.

Take it, but diary the renewal: $7 becomes $20 per seat, and a five-seat team goes from $35 to $100 a month without anyone changing anything. Professional is a different decision entirely and should be treated as one. The $3,000 onboarding fee is required, so the real question is not “is $800 a month worth it” but “is $12,600 in year one worth it”. That buys automation, omni-channel campaigns and reporting that Starter does not have.

If what you actually need is one of those three, price a dedicated tool against it before you commit. A marketing-automation tool and an analytics tool together will usually come in well under $12,600, and the argument for HubSpot has to be the single database, not any one feature. A note on HubSpot AEO, which is new: at $50 a month it tracks how your brand shows up in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity across 25 prompts.

It needs no other subscription, so it is buyable on its own — worth knowing if answer-engine visibility is the only thing you came for.

How to actually use HubSpot

Start on Free and connect one thing at a time. HubSpot's free CRM holds 1,000 contacts and 1 million records across other object types, connects to Gmail, Google Workspace and most versions of Outlook, and takes no card. Two users is the hard limit, so decide early which two. The order that saves money later: import your contacts before you configure anything else, because the contact count is what decides your tier, and a messy import with duplicates inflates it.

Deduplicate first, import second. If you do go paid, buy Starter monthly rather than annually the first time. The promo applies to both, and monthly leaves you free to walk away when you discover which hub you actually use — information you will not have until you have run a quarter on it. And before you say yes to Professional, ask the sales team to put the $3,000 onboarding fee in the quote in writing: it is on the pricing page in a footnote, and a quote that omits it is a quote you will be surprised by.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Sales & CRM software?

Most tools in this category plug in rather than replace. Check which direction the data flows and whether the sync is genuinely two-way, because a one-way push creates a second source of truth that your reps will quietly stop trusting. If it does aim to replace your CRM, price the migration honestly: history, custom fields and reporting rarely move across cleanly.
