What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The App Store listing now shows four flat plans with unlimited orders; logbase.io/selleasy/pricing shows the same plans in its cards and comparison table but still carries a block with the old order-based ladder and the line 'Priced on total store orders, not app orders'. Merchants who installed earlier may still be on the old ladder. Look at the charge Shopify shows at install rather than at either page. The free plan now caps campaigns instead of orders: up to 5, against unlimited from Starter at $9.

Five is enough for a test, not for a store that wants a separate offer on each collection. Starter says '50 free AI recommendations' with no period attached, while Pro says '1,000 AI recommendations / month' and Max '2,500 AI recommendations / month'. The contrast suggests the 50 is a one-off allowance, but neither page says so outright. Ask before you plan around it. And treat the vendor's headline claims as claims: the 20% AOV increase and the 40,000+ brands are Logbase's own figures on its own site, with no methodology attached to either.

What Selleasy does

Shopify upsell and cross-sell app (by Logbase): bundles and product add-ons on the product page, add-on pop-ups and response-based funnels on the cart, one-click post-purchase offers and thank-you page add-ons. Priced by your store's total order count rather than by upsell revenue, and free under 50 orders a month.

What buyers report about Selleasy

Hands-on: not tested by usReported by buyers: Shopify App Store, read September 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run Selleasy ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Support that gets on a call. Reviewers from Poland, Spain, India, Estonia and the UK name individual Logbase staff for Zoom sessions that either set the app up or fixed a broken widget in under fifteen minutes. It is by some distance the most repeated theme on the listing.
  • • Setup speed. A US store reports a Frequently Bought Together offer live within an hour of installing; a Latvian store almost four years in reports cart value up significantly since.
  • • It matches the theme. Styling the widget in the store's own colours comes up repeatedly as the reason it does not read as a bolt-on.
What they complain about
  • • A $0.00 checkout. A US store almost two years in reports an order for a $1,199 product that checked out at zero, shipped and was delivered before they caught it, and says repeated support tickets were met with denial rather than investigation (July 2026, still unresolved when they wrote). This is one merchant's account and Logbase disputes involvement; it is also the most serious thing on the listing, and it concerns an app that sits in the checkout flow.
  • • The meter, again. A US store that left after three days says the app charges on the number of sales your website does, not the number its upsells generate — the same objection our pricing section makes, from someone who paid it (November 2025).
  • • Add-ons do not appear behind Shopify's accelerated checkout. Logbase's public reply confirms it: the 'Buy it now' button sends only the main product and third-party apps cannot change that, so add-ons work with the standard Add to Cart button and not with express checkout.
  • • Pressure after a bad review. The same November 2025 reviewer describes being emailed repeatedly within 24 hours and contacted through their own storefront chat after posting one star; Logbase disputes the count and the route in its public reply. Both sides are on the page.

Where the ratings disagree. 4.9 across 2,705 reviews, with 97% at five stars against 26 one-star and 5 two-star. The average is close to meaningless at that skew and the 26 carry everything worth knowing, so filtering the listing to one and two stars is the ten seconds that pays: it surfaces what the app meters, what it cannot do behind accelerated checkout, and one unresolved checkout incident. One more thing about this listing specifically — Logbase replies in public to nearly every review, positive and negative, which makes the replies a second source. The grandfathered pricing in our Q&A came out of one of them.

What the money actually buys

On Free the money buys nothing because there is none: unlimited orders, 18 strategies, Shopify and manual recommendations, capped at 5 campaigns. Starter at $9 a month buys unlimited campaigns, 50 free AI recommendations and a free setup call. That step is the one most stores will take, and it is cheap. The real feature step is Pro at $29 a month, $295 a year. That is where the AI layer opens (1,000 recommendations a month instead of 50, an AI campaign optimizer, advanced campaign targeting, metafield bulk recommendations) and where checkout upsells for Shopify Plus live.

Max at $59, $601 a year, raises recommendations to 2,500 a month and adds advanced customizations, a rules engine, an AOV advisor and priority support. Before October 2026 those two tiers cost $49 and $99 and appeared only on Logbase's own site. Read on apps.shopify.com/upsell-cross-sell-kit-1 on 30 October 2026.

How to actually use Selleasy

Start on Free: it costs nothing, has no order limit and gives you up to 5 campaigns with all 18 strategies, which is enough to find out whether upsells move anything in your store. Move to Starter at $9 a month only when you need more than five campaigns, or want the 50 free AI recommendations and the free setup call. Before you install, decide which of the six offer types you actually want: bundles and product add-ons on the product page, add-on pop-ups and response-based funnels on the cart, one-click post-purchase offers on the same order, and thank-you page add-ons.

Set up one of them rather than all six and let it run a fortnight; a frequently-bought-together bundle on your best seller is the usual first test. Two things push you to Pro at $29 a month: wanting AI recommendations at volume, and being on Shopify Plus and wanting checkout upsells.

Frequently asked questions

What is Selleasy actually metering?

Since its October 2026 repricing, nothing that grows with your store. Read on apps.shopify.com/upsell-cross-sell-kit-1 on 30 October 2026, every plan says 'Unlimited orders': Free costs nothing and caps you at 5 campaigns, Starter is $9 a month and lifts that to unlimited campaigns, Pro is $29 and Max is $59. The order-count ladder this review used to be built around (free to 50 store orders, then $9, $19 and $29 by order volume) is gone from the listing that does the billing. What still rises with use is the AI allowance: 50 free AI recommendations on Starter, 1,000 a month on Pro and 2,500 a month on Max.

Where do AI recommendations at volume and checkout upsells start?

At Pro, $29 a month or $295 a year, read on the App Store listing on 30 October 2026. Pro adds 1,000 AI recommendations a month, the AI campaign optimizer, checkout upsells for Shopify Plus, advanced campaign targeting and metafield bulk recommendations. Max, $59 a month or $601 a year, adds 2,500 recommendations a month, advanced customizations, a rules engine, an AOV advisor and priority support. Before October Logbase's own site put those two tiers at $49 and $99; the listing now shows all four plans, so the ceiling is no longer hidden.

If the price goes up, am I moved onto the new one?

On the evidence of Logbase's own public answer, no. When a merchant complained on the App Store in November 2025 that the fee had climbed from $8.99 to $16.99 to $19, Logbase replied in public with the account history: that store installed in September 2024, paid $8.99 while it sat in the 51-500 order band, moved to $16.99 once it passed 500 orders a month, and would stay at a $16.99 ceiling — the $19 rate applied only to stores installing from that point on. So two different things were being felt at once: the order band doing what it says, and a list price that new installs pay and existing ones do not. It is a support reply rather than a published policy, so ask for it in writing if the grandfathering is part of your decision.

Does the Thinking Model use up AI credits faster?

Yes. Logbase's pricing page (logbase.io/selleasy/pricing, read 20 October 2026) says using the Thinking Model uses 2 credits per product. The page does not spell out how credits map onto the recommendation allowances (50 free on Starter, 1,000 a month on Pro, 2,500 on Max), so check the in-app counter after a first run before you switch a whole catalogue over.

What happens when the AI recommendations run out?

No surprise bill: the same page says recommendations pause rather than auto-charging. The flip side is that AI picks stop until you upgrade or the allowance resets; what shoppers see in the meantime is not described.
