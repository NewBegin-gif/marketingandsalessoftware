When the paid tier is worth it

The seat ladder is conventional — Foundation $13, Growth $25, Pro $42, Business $59 and Enterprise $79 per user a month on annual billing, with monthly running about 15% higher — but two things about it are unusual and both work in your favour. Unlimited contacts and full API access come with EVERY tier.

Most CRMs ration one or both, and rationing either is how a cheap CRM becomes an expensive one, so integrations are genuinely not the trap here. The add-ons are. Marketing starts at $49 a month and Engagement at $16 per user a month, and that second one changes the arithmetic most at the bottom of the ladder: $16 on top of Foundation's $13 is $29 a seat, more than double.

On Enterprise the same $16 is a 20% increase. So the cheaper your CRM tier, the more the add-on distorts it — and a team that needs Engagement should compare Foundation-plus-Engagement against Growth or Pro on features rather than assuming the cheapest seat stays cheapest.

The marketing side has a second meter underneath the sticker: email and SMS are billed by contact count and message volume. That means the marketing spend scales with your list while the CRM scales with your headcount, and for a small sales team with a large list the marketing side can quietly outgrow the CRM itself.

Model those separately; they do not move together. Two capabilities to check against your requirements before picking a tier, because they sit at the top and nowhere else: SSO and SQL data access are Enterprise-only. If IT will require single sign-on, your tier is decided at $79 regardless of what the sales team needs.

Who it is for

Best for small sales teams that want a friendly CRM with unlimited contacts and full API access on every tier, including the cheapest.

Skip it if you need the marketing suite at scale — Marketing, Engagement, email and SMS are separate add-ons priced by volume, so price those before the seats.

What to weigh it against

The comparison that matters is HubSpot or Pipedrive — the bigger-name CRMs.

Questions buyers actually ask
