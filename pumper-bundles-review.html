Full pricing notes (3 more paragraphs)

Free covers up to $300 of extra revenue generated by the app, counted over the life of the store according to the app. Starter is $9.99 a month, or $94.68 a year (saves 21%), and covers up to $1,000. Growth is $29.99 a month or $269.88 a year (save 25%) and covers up to $5,000. Unlimited is $49.99 a month or $449.88 a year (save 25%) and removes the ceiling.

Where the two differ, the listing is the page Shopify bills from. Neither page defines how the attributed extra revenue that decides your tier is measured. The vendor trades as Profit Pumper (Henq eCommerce LLC, Delaware).

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The bill therefore rises exactly when the app is working, and it rises in steps: pass $300 and you owe $9.99, pass $1,000 and you owe $29.99, pass $5,000 and you owe $49.99. Read that in both directions. It makes every upgrade past the first buy you nothing new, only permission to keep going. The free plan is smaller than the listing suggests: in our own test on 17 November 2026 it left out Buy X Get Y, Mix and Match, upsells and free shipping goals, and the app calls its $300 a lifetime limit.

The one number the pricing table never defines is how that attributed extra revenue is measured, and it is the number the entire ladder rests on.

When the paid plan is worth it

Read the ladder as a take rate and the decision gets simple. Starter is $9.99 once the app has generated $300, Growth $29.99 past $1,000 and Unlimited $49.99 past $5,000, so the share of attributed revenue you hand back falls from about 3% at the Growth threshold to roughly 1% at the Unlimited one. Every upgrade costs less than the revenue that triggered it, which is the whole design. Annual billing takes another slice: $94.68 against $119.88 on Starter, $269.88 against $359.88 on Growth and $449.88 against $599.88 on Unlimited: $25.20, $90 and $150 saved.

Past the first paid step, paying more does not buy a single extra feature: Build, Scale and Dominate, as the app calls them, carry the same list. The first step does, since Free in the app leaves out Buy X Get Y, Mix and Match, upsells, free shipping goals and the countdown timer (our own test, 17 November 2026). Nor does it buy a definition: the attributed-revenue figure that decides your tier is never explained on the listing, so an upgrade settles the invoice without settling the question of how the number was reached.

    How to actually use Pumper Bundles

    Start on Free for volume discounts and bundles, which it covers up to $300 of attributed revenue over the life of the store; Buy X Get Y, Mix and Match, upsells and free shipping goals need a paid plan. Build one offer rather than a catalogue: pick the product where a second unit is plausible and put a quantity break on it using a single discount type, Each Unit, Percentage or Flat, instead of testing all three at once.

    Then check where it renders. The listing names integrations for page builders (GemPages, PageFly) and cart drawers (Upcart, Monster Cart, AMP Slider cart, Opus Cart, qikify), which is another way of saying a themed store with a custom drawer needs verifying rather than assuming: look at the product page and the drawer on a phone before you announce the offer. Write down your own revenue on that product first. Since the ceiling is measured on revenue the app claims to have created, your own before-and-after figure is the only check you hold on it.

    What is the most this can cost, $39.99 or $49.99?

    The listing says $49.99 and the vendor's own FAQ says $39.99, which is the second thing their two pages disagree about. Under 'Are there any hidden charges?' their site answers that Pumper charges a maximum of $39.99 a month and only when it generates meaningful extra revenue. The App Store listing, which is where the charge is actually raised, tops out at the Unlimited tier at $49.99 a month or $449.88 a year. A ten-dollar gap on the ceiling of a four-step ladder suggests one page was updated and the other was not, and the one you should believe is the listing, because Shopify bills from it.

    The paid tiers have the same features. So what am I actually buying?

    Permission for the app to keep earning. On the listing all four tiers ship the identical four items: unlimited bundles and quantity breaks, chat support seven days a week, bundles on page builders, cart drawer integrations, and what changes is a ceiling on the extra revenue the app is allowed to generate: $300 on Free, $1,000 on Starter at $9.99, $5,000 on Growth at $29.99, and no ceiling on Unlimited at $49.99. Read as a take rate that is good news going up: about 3% of the revenue that triggers the Growth step, roughly 1% at the Unlimited one, and every upgrade costs less than the revenue that caused it. Inside the app, in our own test on 17 November 2026, Free is the exception: it leaves out free shipping, swatches, subscriptions, Mix and Match, Buy X Get Y, upsells, the progressive free gift and the countdown timer, which every paid plan includes. So the first upgrade adds offer types, and the upgrades after it add only room.

    How is that 'extra revenue generated by Pumper' measured?

    Neither page says, and it is the number the entire ladder rests on. Both the listing and the vendor's site describe the tiers in terms of extra revenue generated by the app, and the vendor's site adds that its analytics attribute revenue to each offer type so you can see which ones move average order value. What neither states is the rule: whether an order that would have happened anyway counts when a bundle was merely shown, how a partly-returned bundle is treated, or over what window an order stays attributed. Since crossing a threshold is what moves you from $9.99 to $29.99 to $49.99, ask for the definition before you rely on the free tier's $300 as a real allowance.
