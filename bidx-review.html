Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 11 Sep 2026

Amazon PPC is sold in three tiers, and every one of them is a monthly fee plus an unspecified percentage of connected ad spend. They are Self Service Platform €495/month, Managed Platform €1,995/month and Managed Service €4,995/month. The tiers differ mainly in how much of a human you get. That is one monthly strategy call on Self Service, up to two calls and up to 10h of assistance a month on Managed Platform, and up to four calls and up to 32h on Managed Service.

Amazon DSP is priced separately on the same page: Self Service €495/month and Managed Service €995/month, again plus a percentage of ad spend. Amazon Marketing Cloud is sold as reporting: €295 for the default report, custom reports quote-only. The Share of Voice add-on covers the first 100 keywords free and charges $250 a month per additional 500 keywords. All prices are net with VAT added on top, and the page states that plans are based on an annual commitment paid monthly; the FAQ adds that new customers can take a paid three-month proof-of-concept period first.

Walmart Managed Service, high ad-spend volumes and large agencies are contact-sales. Sellers under €10,000 a month in ad spend are pointed to a separate product, BidX Essential, whose own landing page at get.bidx.io/essentials lists $195/month for up to $10K in monthly ad spend — a flat fee with no percentage attached. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Every Amazon PPC and DSP tier ends with “+ % of connected ad spend per month”, and that percentage appears nowhere — not on the tier cards, not in the footnotes, not in the FAQ. So €495 is a floor, not a price, and nobody spending real money on Amazon ads can work out their monthly cost from this page. Ask for the percentage in writing, and ask whether it is charged on all connected spend or only on what BidX manages, before you get as far as a contract.

The second thing to price is the commitment. The page states plans run on an annual commitment paid monthly, and the FAQ confirms it: the commitment is annual, and the only shorter path on offer is a paid three-month proof of concept. There is no free trial. That makes the cheapest visible door €5,940 a year net of VAT before a cent of the percentage — a very different proposition from the €495 the eye lands on.

Two smaller inconsistencies worth noticing, because they tell you how carefully the price list is maintained. On a page priced in euros, the one add-on with a hard number is quoted in dollars ($250 per extra 500 keywords), so ask which currency you are actually billed in. And BidX Essential — the $195/month product they route smaller sellers to — lives on a completely separate landing page carrying a 2024 copyright line and a different phone number.

It is not listed among the tiers on the pricing page itself, so confirm it still exists at that price before you plan around it.

What BidX does

Amazon-advertising automation from German vendor BidX GmbH: bid and budget automation, campaign creation, dayparting and target-ACOS rules for Sponsored Products, Brands and Display, with Amazon DSP and Marketing Cloud reporting and organic-plus-paid share-of-voice tracking in the same dashboard.

What buyers report about BidX

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in Capterra August 2026; OMR Reviews September 2026. We have not run BidX ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Rule-based bid automation and campaign creation are the core of it: sellers say building campaign structures across several marketplaces at once is far faster than doing it in Amazon's own interface, and scheduling — pausing campaigns at set times or on set weekdays — gets named specifically.
  • • Total-ACOS targeting and a monthly call with an account manager who takes feature feedback come up repeatedly, including from a wholesale team five years in.
  • • On OMR Reviews, where BidX carries 4.7 out of 5 across 45 reviews (read September 2026), the recurring praise is the same automation of bids and campaign creation, plus personal support: responsive account managers and reachable support channels are named repeatedly, and reviewers credit the platform with bringing ACoS down.
What they complain about
  • • The dashboard is the recurring weak spot, named by separate reviewers across three years.
  • • Gaps around the data layer: reviewers ask for CSV export or a data interface and for deeper integration with external tracking tools, and one (November 2022) wanted campaigns paused automatically when stock runs low — described then as on the roadmap, and we did not verify whether it shipped.
  • • Settings and reports are not self-explanatory; several reviewers describe a short but real onboarding before it clicks.
  • • Speed is the complaint that only shows up on OMR Reviews: long loading times, named specifically for large product catalogues, alongside occasional bugs and site instability.
  • • Reporting and bulk work come up on OMR Reviews too — limited real-time reporting, thin bulk editing, and adjustment logs that arrive as one large file rather than broken down per automation or per campaign, which is the same data-layer gap Capterra reviewers describe.

Where the ratings disagree. The two platforms disagree about the dashboard, and that is the thing to test in a demo. On Capterra it is the recurring weak spot, named by separate reviewers across three years; on OMR Reviews it is praised for clarity and for being customisable. Volume and recency explain part of it: Capterra shows a clean 5.0 but across only nine reviews, mostly German-speaking Amazon sellers, with the newest from May 2025; OMR Reviews shows 4.7 across 45, with the newest inside the last twelve months. Nine reviews cannot show you the failure modes, and a record that stops a year back cannot tell you about the current product — so where the two sets differ, the larger and fresher one is describing a product the smaller one never saw.

When the paid plan is worth it

Start with what the ladder does not tell you: every tier is a monthly fee PLUS a percentage of connected ad spend, and that percentage is not published anywhere on the page. None of the numbers below are therefore a complete price, and the percentage is the first thing to get in writing because it scales with your budget rather than with your plan. What the tiers actually differ on is how much of a human you get, and that makes the step cost calculable.

Self Service is EUR 495 a month with one monthly strategy call. Managed Platform is EUR 1,995 with up to two calls and up to 10 hours of assistance -- EUR 1,500 more for 10 hours, which is EUR 150 an hour. Managed Service is EUR 4,995 with up to four calls and up to 32 hours: EUR 3,000 more than Managed Platform for 22 extra hours, or about EUR 136 an hour.

You are buying agency time at roughly EUR 140 an hour, so compare it against what an Amazon PPC freelancer costs you rather than against the software. If your ad spend is under EUR 10,000 a month the page points you at a different product entirely: BidX Essential, at $195 a month for up to $10K in monthly spend -- and that one is a flat fee with no percentage attached.

For a smaller seller that is not a cheaper tier, it is a different pricing model, and it is the one to ask about first. Everything is net of VAT and plans run on an annual commitment paid monthly, though the FAQ says new customers can take a paid three-month proof of concept first.

How to actually use BidX

Registration and then the API authorisation is effectively the whole setup: you link the Amazon Advertising Console and Seller Central accounts from inside BidX. Only the last seven days of performance data load at first, and BidX says the upload takes about 30 minutes; once the Advertising API is connected, 60 days become available to consult. Their guide tells you to check the spam folder if the confirmation mail does not arrive.

From there you build a dashboard, define ad segments, and add automations and individual rules — their own advice is to start with simple rules so you can see what each one does.

Common BidX setup mistakes

Where BidX setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

The thing teams get wrong is expecting the automation to act immediately.

  • A keyword is only adjusted once roughly 15 clicks have accumulated, so a thin account has nothing for it to act on.
  • After an adjustment BidX waits three days before touching that keyword again, to allow for the time Amazon needs to attribute sales fully.
  • Individual rules are checked daily and the adjustments are made after midnight.
