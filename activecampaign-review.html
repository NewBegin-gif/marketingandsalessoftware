ActiveCampaign Review (2026)

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

Pricing climbs with contact count and the add-ons (CRM, SMS, extra seats) stack up fast — the sticker Starter price is not what a serious automation setup actually costs. Powerful, but budget for the tier (Plus and up) you'll really use. Throughout this review we weigh it against Mailchimp.

Best for

businesses that want deep marketing automation and lifecycle email tied to a light CRM, and will actually use the automation depth.

Skip it if

you just need to send a newsletter — a simpler, cheaper email tool does that without paying for automation you won't touch.

We tested it · 2026-09-30

On 30 September 2026 we opened a new ActiveCampaign account on the 14-day free trial, which carries the Pro plan's features, and gave Active Intelligence 3.0: Wavelength ten tasks: a brand kit from our website, a campaign from one of our test pages (twice), a Dutch translation, an image, a welcome automation, a daily scheduled task, industry benchmarks and an automation health check. We checked every result against our own pages, saved before it read them.

  • On the empty account it said plainly that there were no campaigns in the past 12 months and no sends to compare with benchmarks, instead of inventing a best performer or figures.
  • Given one of our test pages, it wrote a complete campaign in about three minutes. All ten figures in it came from the page and none was added. The same prompt in a new conversation produced a different layout with one sentence the page does not support.
  • The Brand Kit it built from our homepage matched our voice, mission and colours, but it saved a blue 'N' logo that is not ours, which then appeared in every email, and it picked products we review as competitors.

Your email send allowance is a multiple of your contact count — 10x on Starter and Plus, 12x on Pro, 15x on Enterprise. What buyers report about ActiveCampaign

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We tested ActiveCampaign ourselves on 2026-09-30, but one test is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The automation builder is still the deepest in its price range, and that is what keeps reviewers on it despite everything below.
  • • CRM and email in one place, with a low entry price.
What they complain about
  • • Accounts created on or after 3 November 2025 are billed for all contacts — including unsubscribed, bounced and unconfirmed. That is the single most important thing to know here: your bill grows with a list you are not mailing. Older accounts should check which basis theirs is on.
  • • Crossing a contact tier re-prices the whole plan, not just the excess. Reviewers describe renewal jumps they had not budgeted for.
  • • Charges after a confirmed cancellation, unauthorised charges after closing an account, and difficult refunds are all recurring.
  • • Support leans on AI chat and reviewers report struggling to reach a person.
  • • Mobile rendering is inconsistent and you cannot design a separate mobile layout.
  • • TrustRadius is where the day-to-day friction gets named, and in July 2026 a seven-year user listed it precisely: a minimum five-minute wait on every automation condition, a confusing split between archiving, deleting and excluding contacts, merging two addresses for one contact not merging their lists, double opt-in that cannot be triggered from external tools such as webinar platforms, and a new mail designer they say never works.
  • • The same platform carries the other 2026 complaint: too much AI in the interface, with AI messages covering information and taking up space, and AI recommendations that ignore what you already did.
  • • Even the positive reviews say it plainly: this is not a tool for beginners.

Where the ratings disagree. Trustpilot has sat between 2.8 and 3.0 across roughly 1,300-1,400 reviews through 2026 — low for this category — while the software directories rate it well. The directories are rating the automation; Trustpilot is rating the invoice.

When the paid plan is worth it

There is no free plan: after the 14-day trial (no credit card required) you pay, and ActiveCampaign backs that with a 30-day money-back guarantee. Judge the tiers on the feature grid rather than the price list, because the price list is rendered client-side and only appears in a real browser. Every tier on activecampaign.com/pricing does carry a figure, but a plain fetch retrieves none of them — and neither does our own monthly capture of that page.

If a comparison site tells you ActiveCampaign does not publish its prices, that is what it is looking at. On the published grid the sharpest break is automation depth: Starter is listed at 5 actions per automation, while Plus, Pro and Enterprise are listed as unlimited automation actions. That is the concrete thing you buy by leaving Starter. What you do not buy is a seat: Starter and Plus are both 1 user, Pro is 3 and Enterprise 5.

And you do not buy a CRM either. Pipelines and Sales Engagement are separately priced add-ons, listed by Prospeo at $49 and $85 per month on top of your plan. Sending headroom moves slowly as well: the monthly send cap is 10x your contact limit on Starter and Plus, 12x on Pro and 15x on Enterprise. Billing is per contact regardless of how little you send, so a broadcast-only list gets expensive on volume alone.

On the vendor's own page on 29 September 2026, 1,000 contacts on annual billing is $15 for Starter, $49 for Plus, $79 for Pro and $145 for Enterprise a month; at 10,000 contacts the same four read $149, $189, $375 and $589. Prospeo lists Starter at $19 per month if you pay month-to-month.

Its task description asked for Brand Kit mentions on those sites, which differed from our request for competitor news.

How to actually use ActiveCampaign

First value is a campaign, not an automation: import a list, pick a template, send. That is a same-day job. Before your first real send, authenticate your sending domain in ActiveCampaign's settings and add the DNS records it gives you at your DNS host. If you are migrating in, ActiveCampaign says its free migration service moves lists, tags and custom fields, email templates and forms, and up to 10 automations, and that it includes account setup help; the page names no plan-tier or paid condition.

Treat that as the vendor's own description of its own service and check it against your setup before you rely on it — the list stops at 10 automations and says nothing about third-party integrations. For the builder itself, ActiveCampaign says it ships 950+ automation recipes, which is a cheaper starting point than a blank canvas; that count is also the vendor's, not an independent one. Check your contact count early.

SendX reports that on accounts created on or after 3 November 2025, unsubscribed, unconfirmed and bounced contacts all count toward the limit you are billed for, while accounts created before that date are grandfathered onto the old rule and pay for active contacts only. SendX also says the change was never publicly announced, and ActiveCampaign's pricing page does not state it, so confirm which rule applies to you against your own invoice.

Frequently asked questions

What does ActiveCampaign cost?

On annual billing the published starting points are $15 for Starter, $49 for Plus, $79 for Pro and $145 for Enterprise a month, read on 17 August 2026 and unchanged on 29 September 2026. Those are floors: the real figure depends on your contact count, and the page only shows it after you choose one — at 10,000 contacts it read $149, $189, $375 and $589. There is no free plan.

Which tier do I need, and what actually separates them?

Three things rather than a long feature list: how many users (1, 1, 3 and 5), how deep the segmentation goes (limited, standard, advanced, premium) and how many emails you may send — a multiple of your contacts, 10x on Starter and Plus, 12x on Pro, 15x on Enterprise. The one hard wall low down is Starter's cap of five actions per automation, which rules out anything beyond a simple sequence.

What does Active Intelligence 3.0: Wavelength add, and which plan gets it?

Active Intelligence 3.0: Wavelength is ActiveCampaign's first named AI release, published in its release notes on 27 August 2026. The AI now works from your account's own history, sends and automations; it can draft a campaign before you ask, turn any prompt into a daily, weekly or monthly scheduled task, watch your website and the competitor brands in your Brand Kit, and translate a campaign into 75+ languages. What the release notes do not say is which plan gets which part, and ActiveCampaign's partner notes warn that availability "may vary by account and capability". The pricing table, read on 30 September 2026, is the better guide: the Active Intelligence workspace is unlimited on Pro and Enterprise but "Limited access" on Plus and Starter, with no figure for the limit; AI content and image generation are not included on Starter; Starter gets one AI brand kit against five on the other plans; and AI translations and AI actions are listed as paid add-ons on all four tiers. So the $15 Starter price buys little of Active Intelligence 3.0: Wavelength, and translation costs extra whichever plan you choose.

What should I check before committing?

Two things reviewers keep getting caught by. First, which side of a date your account falls on: the billing rule that counts unsubscribed, bounced and unconfirmed contacts is written for accounts created on or after 3 November 2025, so ask whether an older account on the same plan is counted the same way before you assume a quoted figure applies to you. Second, what happens at a tier boundary — crossing a contact tier re-prices the whole plan rather than just the excess, which is the renewal jump people describe. Ask both questions before you import a list.
