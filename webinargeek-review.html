WebinarGeek Review (2026)No Basic Plan, Premium From €59/mo

By Daan Haket · Tested hands-on · written with an AI assistant · who does what

The friendly €59 headline is the annual rate for 75 viewers, and the real cost lives in add-ons: localisation, the AI assistant, CRM integrations, RTMP and branding profiles are each €20-59 a month on top, and only a Premium subscription can buy them.

Best for

marketing and sales teams running live and automated webinars in Europe who want a polished, GDPR-friendly platform with viewer-tier pricing.

Skip it if

you run occasional small webinars (your video-call tool may cover it), or you need the CRM and localization features that sit behind paid add-ons.

When the paid plan is worth it

WebinarGeek has no free plan, only a 14-day trial with every Premium feature, so the real question is which viewer tier you buy rather than whether to pay. Read on 3 October 2026, the pricing page no longer sells a Basic plan: Premium is the entry point, priced per viewer tier at €59 a month billed annually or €99 monthly for 75 viewers, then €79 against €129 at 125, €119 against €199 at 250, €179 against €299 at 500 and €239 against €399 at 1,000.

Annual billing now takes about 40% off at every tier, so the advertised "save up to 40%" holds across the ladder rather than only on the top rung. What upgrading does not solve is demand, because capacity is bought per tier rather than per attendee: an over-bought tier is money sitting idle and an under-bought one fails on the day. Nor does it fold in the depth. The add-ons are sold as extensions of a Premium subscription, so localisation and the AI assistant at €20 each, branding profiles at €59, HubSpot and Salesforce at €49 each and RTMP at €29.50 are all billed on top — including the AI assistant, which the vendor says now runs on on-demand webinars as well as live ones (vendor mail, 8 September 2026).

Enterprise, annual-only from €349 at 500 viewers to €849 at 5,000, includes localisation, RTMP, extra users and both CRM integrations, but still lists the AI assistant and branding profiles as add-ons.

What we found running WebinarGeek ourselves

Hands-on · 2026-10-21

The first findings are at the top of this page. The rest:

  • New webinar offers Start from scratch, Generate (AI), Copy or Add to series; Present live, Automated or On-demand; and Public, Unlisted or Private access. 'Offer replay' is on by default, and 'Create and customize' does not publish yet. The title field ignored the first title we typed.
  • Polls sit under Interactions, next to Call to action, Quiz and Information: single or multiple choice, with a default duration of 1:30, and at the start you choose 'End manually' or 'End after a set time'.
  • The studio did not open until we allowed browser notifications and then camera and microphone. In the dry run it said 'Only team members and invited test viewers can join' and 'Dry runs aren't recorded and don't collect statistics'. Slides changed straight away with the arrow keys, and ending the dry run reset all interactions.
  • The viewer link of the unpublished webinar returned 'Not yet published: Publish your webinar first before you can use the registration page.', so a test viewer can only watch after you publish.

How to actually use WebinarGeek

The quickest route to something real is not a blank webinar but the demo webinars WebinarGeek puts in every new account, "My first webinar" and "My first on demand webinar", which can be opened, edited and even used live. The vendor's own guide then runs 11 steps, and the ones that actually gate a launch are Publish and the dry run. The documentation is explicit that publishing is the moment a registration link becomes active: select the webinar, go to "Publish" in the left sidebar and click Publish before promoting anything.

The dry run sits behind the "Dry run" button, is not recorded, and stops automatically after 5 hours of broadcasting. The docs also set connectivity floors: about 2 Mbps upload for presenters and 0.5 Mbps download for viewers, and viewers cannot enter the room until 5 minutes before the scheduled start. In our own test on 21 October 2026 the new account came with three sample webinars marked 'Sample' (a live, an automated and an on-demand one), in Dutch like the rest of the account; English had to be set both in the studio and under Account > Settings > Language.

Publish before you ask a colleague to watch as a test viewer: the viewer link of an unpublished webinar only says 'Not yet published'. And check every poll option before you go live, because an answer that had not saved went live as an empty option, without a warning.

Frequently asked questions

Does WebinarGeek's trial require a credit card?

Sign-up check 2026-10-21: card not requested in this account; trial length confirmed: 14 days.

What does WebinarGeek cost?

There is no Basic plan any more; Premium is the entry point. Billed annually it is €59 a month at 75 viewers, €79 at 125, €119 at 250, €179 at 500 and €239 at 1,000; billed monthly €99, €129, €199, €299 and €399. Enterprise is annual-only, from €349 at 500 viewers up to €849 at 5,000. The page offers several currencies and prices by region, so confirm yours at checkout — the figures render client-side and do not appear in a plain fetch. Read on 3 October 2026.

What is not included in the plan price?

The add-ons, each billed on top of a Premium subscription, per month on annual billing: extra users €10, localisation €20, the AI assistant €20, branding profiles €59 (an extra workspace and e-mail domain included), HubSpot €49, Salesforce €49 and RTMP streaming €29.50, plus a one-off €495 for online onboarding. A team that wants CRM sync and a second brand profile is adding over €100 a month to the plan. Read on 3 October 2026.

Which tier do I need for my audience size?

Viewer count is the meter: Premium steps through 75, 125, 250, 500 and 1,000 concurrent viewers, and Enterprise runs 500 to 5,000. Mind the overlap — at 1,000 viewers Premium is €239 a month on annual billing and Enterprise €449, so that €210 gap buys the Enterprise feature set (custom domain, 12-hour sessions, RTMP, workspaces, three account users and the Salesforce, HubSpot and localisation add-ons included) rather than more capacity. Size on the largest webinar you realistically expect, because the cap is what fails on the day.
