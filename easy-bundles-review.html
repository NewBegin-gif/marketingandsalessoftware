Four tiers billed in USD every 30 days, and the meter is the part that matters: you are charged on bundle sales, not on orders, products or staff. The free plan is free to install and covers up to $500 of bundle sales a month. Essential is $29 a month or $299 a year and lifts that to $3,000; Growth is $49 or $499 a year up to $5,000; Scale is $99 or $999 a year up to $10,000.

Annual billing saves 14% to 16% depending on the tier, and every paid tier has a 15-day free trial. Features climb the same ladder: free gives mix-and-match, fixed and volume bundles with templates and a custom or template design; Essential adds the single-step and multi-step bundle builders, landing-page templates, search and filtering, and multi-currency and multi-language support; Growth adds advanced discount logic, custom personalisation fields, add-ons, free gifts and subscription bundles; Scale adds AI-generated designs, advanced analytics, one-to-one onboarding and an account manager on Slack.

Two things the block does not say are worth noting before you commit. It does not publish what happens when you pass $10,000 of bundle sales in a month, and it does not publish an overage rate; the plan table simply ends there and links out to see all pricing options. What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

A revenue meter reads as generous at the bottom — $500 of bundle sales a month free is a real trial, not a crippled one — but it means every tier upgrade is triggered by the app working, and the published ladder stops at $10,000 a month with no stated overage. A store doing well on bundles has to ask what comes next rather than read it, and that is the wrong moment to be negotiating.

Maturity is not the concern here: the app has been on the App Store since 15 September 2022 and carried 4.9 stars over 1,216 reviews on 4 September 2026 and 1,298 on 19 September, at the same average. The concern is where the money goes as you succeed. Two smaller things: the features most merchants picture when they think bundles are all paid: the guided single- and multi-step build-your-own-box builders sit on Essential at $29, and subscription bundles, add-ons and free gifts on Growth at $49 — none of them on the free plan, and the recent reviews that praise the app most warmly also praise the support team by name, which is a compliment to people rather than to a product that never needs them.

What Easy Bundles does

Shopify bundle builder for build-your-own-box, gift-box, mix-and-match, fixed-price, volume and subscription bundles, with bundle inventory synced across Shopify and 3PLs and POS support.

What buyers report about Easy Bundles

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in September 2026. We have not run Easy Bundles ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Mix-and-match is the feature merchants say they could not find elsewhere: letting the customer take a few items from a bundle rather than all of them. One US store calls it the best app for mix-and-match by far, another says it has features no other bundle app offered.
  • • Support is named person by person — Bijoy, Salaj, Usman, Hiral — and described as replying instantly and making changes on their own end. One merchant reports Bijoy spending an hour and a half building the bundle they had in mind after they could not get it working alone.
  • • Merchants say the bundle looks and behaves better for customers than the alternatives they tried, with several ways to customise the appearance.
  • • The long-tenure reviews are the more informative ones: a UK store at nearly three years says bugs and questions get investigated and sorted quickly, which is a claim about the support queue over time rather than at install.
What they complain about
  • • Discount stacking is a named, missing control: a UK merchant reports there is no way to stop store-wide discounts combining with the bundle discount, which is a margin problem rather than a cosmetic one.
  • • It does not sync with TikTok Shop, reported by a US store that otherwise likes it.
  • • Merchants moving from Fly Bundles — the same developer's other app — say this one is less user-friendly, and one withholds the fifth star for exactly that reason.
  • • A recurring undertone in the five-star reviews is that setup took support to complete: 'a lot of back and forth with the customer service team in order for it to work as we needed'. The support is fast, but needing it appears to be common.

Where the ratings disagree. The 4.9 is real but it is largely a rating of the first hour. Scanning the tenure line under each review, a striking share of the most enthusiastic ones come from merchants who had the app for 37 minutes, about an hour, about two hours or a handful of days — they are rating the onboarding and the support chat, not the app in production. The reviews from merchants at ten months, a year and nearly three years exist and are also positive, but they are far outnumbered. So the number tells you reliably that you will be helped quickly when you install; it tells you much less than its size suggests about the app after a year.

When the paid plan is worth it

The meter reads bundle sales, not orders or SKUs, so your tier is arithmetic you can do before you install: $500 a month free, $3,000 on Essential at $29, $5,000 on Growth at $49, $10,000 on Scale at $99, with annual billing saving 14% to 16% and a 15-day trial on every paid tier. Two of those steps buy capability rather than headroom. Essential unlocks the single-step and multi-step builders — the guided build-your-own-box most merchants actually picture when they say bundle — plus landing pages and multi-currency selling.

Growth adds subscription bundles, add-ons, free gifts and advanced discount logic, so a subscribe-and-save box puts you there whatever your revenue says. What upgrading does not fix is the top of the ladder. The published tiers stop at $10,000 of bundle sales a month and no overage rate is named, so the plan you buy answers every question except the one success creates. Nor does a higher tier improve the plumbing: real-time inventory sync, bundle items arriving at checkout as individual line items for your 3PL, and POS parity all come from Shopify's Cart Transformation API and work the same on the free plan as on Scale.

How to actually use Easy Bundles

Choose the format before the discount. The app splits into full-page experiences, single-step or multi-step, and product-page embeds that sit inline or expand, and that choice decides where shoppers meet the bundle. The vendor runs each format live on a real store in its own gallery, so click through those first and copy the one that matches how you sell, rather than discovering the difference after you have built it.

If you are on a paid plan, take the setup call. Skai Lama offers to build your first bundle with you on your own theme, and the second bundle is a copy of the first, so that hour compounds. The vendor says most stores publish a first bundle within an hour using the no-code builder and theme-matched templates. Then place one test order before you launch. Bundle items reach checkout as separate line items through Shopify's Cart Transformation API, and that is exactly what your 3PL and your subscription app — Recharge, Loop or Appstle — have to read correctly.

Distilled from Easy Bundle Builder vendor product page and its FAQ (skailama.com/shopify-bundle-builder), plus the Shopify App Store listing (apps.shopify.com/bundle-builder), read 4 September 2026 — tiers and the bundle-sales meter, bundle formats, the Cart Transformation API behaviour at checkout, inventory sync and onboarding. The vendor's own helpdesk was not usable as a source: on that date help.skailama.com listed no Easy Bundles category and every Easy Bundles article returned Page Not Found.

The natural comparison is Shopify's own Bundles app — Shopify ships a free, first-party bundles app that handles fixed and mix-and-match bundles with no meter on your revenue, and for a store that wants one simple bundle that is the cheaper answer and always will be. Easy Bundles is worth the $29 to $99 when the bundle is an experience rather than a SKU — a guided build-your-own-box or gift-box flow, subscription bundles, add-ons and free gifts, bundle landing pages in several currencies and languages — and when you want the bundle stock to stay in step with your 3PL without anyone reconciling it by hand.

Frequently asked questions

Which Easy Bundles plan do I need?

Work it out twice and take the higher answer, because two different things move you up the ladder. The first is arithmetic you can do before installing: the meter counts bundle sales, not orders or SKUs, and as read on 19 September 2026 the free plan covers up to $500 of bundle sales a month, Essential $3,000 at $29 a month, Growth $5,000 at $49 and Scale $10,000 at $99. The second is capability, and it does not track revenue at all. The guided single-step and multi-step builders — the build-your-own-box most merchants actually picture when they say bundle — start on Essential, along with landing-page templates, search and filtering and multi-currency selling. Subscription bundles, add-ons, free gifts and advanced discount logic start on Growth. So a store doing $400 a month in subscribe-and-save boxes belongs on Growth at $49 whatever its revenue says, while a store doing $2,500 in plain mix-and-match bundles can sit on Essential. Every paid tier has a 15-day free trial, so you can test the builder before the meter matters.

What happens when I pass $10,000 of bundle sales in a month?

The listing does not say, and that is the one gap worth closing before you commit. Read on 19 September 2026, the plan table ends at Scale — $99 a month, up to $10,000 in bundle sales — and then simply stops, with a link to see all pricing options and no published overage rate, no next tier and no statement of what the app does when you cross the line. Whether bundles keep selling, stop discounting or quietly keep running while an invoice grows is not answerable from the page. This is an ordinary question to put to the vendor in writing, and the right time is while you are still on the free plan, not the month you cross $10,000. Ask three things: what the charge is above $10,000, what happens to live bundles if you do nothing, and whether an annual plan can be moved up mid-term — because the annual discount is exactly what makes an outgrown tier awkward.

Is the annual plan worth taking?

The discount is real but small, and the meter is the reason to think twice. As read on 19 September 2026, Essential is $29 a month or $299 a year, Growth $49 or $499, Scale $99 or $999 — which works out at roughly 14%, 15% and 16% off twelve monthly payments, or about seven weeks free on the top tier. Against that, your tier is set by how much you sell through bundles, and the whole point of installing this app is to make that number go up. Committing a year to Essential is committing to a $3,000-a-month ceiling you are actively trying to break, and the listing does not publish what moving up mid-term costs. The sensible order is to spend the 15-day trial and a couple of monthly cycles finding out which tier your bundle revenue actually settles on, then buy the year on that tier. The saving is still there in month three.

What should I weigh before buying E-commerce software?

Check your platform and version specifically. Shopify support is close to universal in this category, while WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento and headless setups vary a lot, and 'supported' sometimes means a generic API rather than a maintained app. A listing in your own platform's app store, with recent reviews there, is a better signal than the vendor's logo wall.
